Does your business need a fractional CFO? Signs, scope and what to expect

Most growing businesses reach a point where the accountant's monthly visit is no longer enough, but a full-time CFO is still too much. A fractional CFO fills that gap: senior finance leadership for an agreed number of days each month.

By Ryan Vaz, FCA · 19 October 2026 · 5 min read

Signs it is time

  • Monthly accounts arrive late, or the owners do not trust the numbers enough to act on them.
  • Cash is tight despite good sales, and nobody can say with confidence what the balance will be in eight weeks.
  • The business is taking on bank finance, renewing limits, or speaking to investors, and needs projections and reporting that will stand up to questions.
  • Margins have slipped and it is not clear which products, customers or branches are responsible.
  • The owners are spending evenings on finance and compliance instead of the business.
  • A move to new accounting software or an ERP is planned.

Accountant, auditor and fractional CFO

An accountant records transactions and keeps compliance up to date. A statutory auditor gives an independent opinion on the financial statements and, to stay independent, cannot take on management roles for the same client. A fractional CFO works inside the business as part of management, looking forward rather than back: cash, planning, controls, systems and the conversation with lenders and investors. The three roles complement each other and should not all sit with one firm.

A monthly reporting pack that works

Owners do not need forty pages. A useful monthly pack fits on a few pages and answers the same questions every month:

  • Profit for the month and year to date, against budget and last year, with a short explanation of the main differences
  • Cash position today and forecast for the next 13 weeks
  • Receivables by age and by customer, and payables due
  • Inventory levels and slow-moving stock, where relevant
  • Gross margin by product, customer group or branch
  • Status of statutory compliance: GST, TDS, PF, ESI and any notices

How engagements are usually structured

Most fractional CFO arrangements are a fixed number of days per month, with a monthly review meeting with the owners and availability for questions in between. The scope is reviewed every few months as the business changes. Much of the work can be done remotely with cloud accounting and shared files, with in-person meetings when they add value.

Common questions

How many days a month does a fractional CFO usually work?

It varies with the size and needs of the business, often two to six days a month. The first few months usually need more time while reporting and controls are set up.

Can a fractional CFO deal with banks on our behalf?

A fractional CFO typically prepares projections, CMA data and lender information and attends meetings with management. Final commitments and signatures remain with the owners or authorised signatories.

Is a fractional CFO only for startups?

No. Many established, owner-run businesses use fractional CFOs when they grow, borrow more or prepare for succession, as well as startups preparing to raise funds.
