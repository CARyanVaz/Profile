TDS and TCS for tax year 2026-27: rates, defaults and corrections

TDS rates and limits under section 393, with answers on defaults, corrections and the move to the new Act. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Status as on 9 November 2026.

Questions on TDS and TCS

What are the applicable TDS provisions for professional fees?

Under section 393(1) (formerly section 194J), tax is deducted at 10% where fees exceed Rs 50,000 in the year. The rate is 2% for technical services other than professional services, call centres and certain royalties. An individual or HUF not liable to tax audit may be outside this provision, unless a separate one applies.

When is TDS applicable on payments to contractors?

Where a single payment exceeds Rs 30,000, or total payments in the year exceed Rs 1 lakh (formerly section 194C). The rate is 1% for an individual or HUF contractor and 2% for others, on work including manpower supply, as clarified from 2026. Transporters with up to 10 goods carriages who give PAN and a declaration are excluded. Payments for personal purposes by individuals are also excluded.

What are the TDS provisions applicable to commission and brokerage?

Tax is deducted at 2% where commission or brokerage exceeds Rs 20,000 in the year (formerly section 194H). An individual or HUF not liable to tax audit is generally not required to deduct under this head, unless the separate provision for large payments applies. Insurance commission has its own provision.

What is the TDS treatment of rent paid by individuals and businesses?

Businesses deduct 10% on land, buildings and furniture, and 2% on plant and machinery, where rent exceeds Rs 50,000 a month (formerly section 194-I). An individual or HUF not under audit deducts 2% where rent exceeds Rs 50,000 a month, using PAN-based Form 141 without a TAN (formerly section 194-IB). Rent to a non-resident landlord falls under section 393(2) with no such limit.

What are the TDS implications on purchase of immovable property?

A buyer from a resident deducts 1% where the consideration is Rs 50 lakh or more, excluding agricultural land (formerly section 194-IA). The tax is deposited with Form 141 within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction, and no TAN is needed. If stamp duty value is higher, the rate applies on that value. Buying from a non-resident is different: see NRI property TDS.

What are the TDS provisions for purchase of goods?

A buyer whose turnover exceeded Rs 10 crore in the preceding year deducts 0.1% on purchases from a resident seller above Rs 50 lakh in the year, on the excess over Rs 50 lakh (formerly section 194Q). If the seller has no PAN, the rate is 5%. Sale of goods TCS under the old section 206C(1H) ended from 1 April 2025.

What are the consequences of non-deduction or short deduction of TDS?

The payer can be treated as an assessee in default for the tax not deducted, with interest of 1% a month or part of a month. A business also loses 30% of a payment to a resident as a deduction, and all of a payment to a non-resident, until the tax is paid. Penalty and prosecution can apply in serious cases. Relief is available if the payee has included the income and paid tax, with an accountant's certificate.

Can a deductor claim relief for TDS deposited late?

Interest of 1.5% a month or part of a month must still be paid, and is not generally waived. Business expenses disallowed for late deposit are allowed if the tax is paid by the return due date, or otherwise in the year of payment. A long delay can lead to prosecution, so pay the tax and interest as soon as the default is found.

What is the procedure for correction of TDS returns?

Download the consolidated file from TRACES, correct PAN, challan or amount details with the return preparation utility, validate, and upload it. Quarters to March 2026 use the old forms, and later quarters use the new ones. One source says correction is now limited to two years from the end of the year in which the original statement was due, so check the portal early.

How should excess TDS or TCS be adjusted or refunded?

A payee claims excess tax as a refund in the income tax return. A deductor who deposited too much can use the unconsumed balance against later liabilities through a correction statement, or apply for a refund through TRACES where it was paid by mistake. The money cannot be taken back once it has been credited to the payee.

What are the implications of incorrect PAN or non-furnishing of PAN?

Tax is generally deducted at 20%, or the applicable rate if higher, and 5% in some cases such as purchase of goods. An inoperative PAN is treated in the same way. The payee can lose credit too, because credit follows Form 26AS. The separate higher rate for non-filers under the old sections 206AB and 206CCA has been removed.

What are the consequences of late filing of TDS statements?

A late fee of Rs 200 a day applies, capped at the tax in the statement, and it must be paid before the statement is filed. A penalty can also apply for late or incorrect statements. Statements are due on 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 31 May, and certificates depend on them, so delay also affects payees.

How are TDS provisions applied to payments credited before 1 April 2026 but paid afterwards?

The Act is decided by the earlier of credit or payment. If the amount was credited on or before 31 March 2026, the 1961 Act applies, with the old section codes, forms and certificates, even if it was paid later. New forms apply only where the earlier event is on or after 1 April 2026. Do not deduct twice on one amount.

Which forms are used for quarterly TDS and TCS statements from tax year 2026-27?

For quarters from April 2026, quarterly statements are filed in Form 138 for TDS on salary (formerly Form 24Q), Form 140 for TDS on other payments to residents (formerly Form 26Q), Form 144 for payments to non-residents (formerly Form 27Q) and Form 143 for TCS (formerly Form 27EQ). Statements for quarters up to March 2026, including corrections, stay in the old forms. The due dates are 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 31 May.

What does “Has the statement been filed earlier for this quarter?” mean in a TDS return?

The return preparation utility asks whether a regular statement for the same TAN, form and quarter has already been filed. For the first statement of that quarter, answer No. If a statement for the quarter was filed and accepted, a fresh regular statement is not the way to change it; download the consolidated file from TRACES and file a correction statement quoting the token number (provisional receipt number) of the original. A separate question asks whether a regular statement was filed for an earlier period; answer Yes and give that statement's token number if one was.
