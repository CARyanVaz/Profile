TDS when an NRI sells property in Mumbai, and how to reduce it

When a non-resident sells a flat in Mumbai, the buyer must deduct tax from the price. Because the deduction is on the full sale value rather than the gain, it is often far more than the tax owed. Planning before the sale deed avoids locking up that money for months. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Status as on 9 November 2026.

Why TDS is high when the seller is an NRI

For a resident seller, the buyer deducts 1 per cent only where the price is ₹50 lakh or more. For a non-resident seller there is no threshold. The buyer must deduct tax at the rate that applies to the non-resident's capital gain, on the whole amount paid, under section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 195). Because the deduction is on the full price rather than the gain, it is often far more than the tax actually owed.

What the buyer must do

  • Deduct tax at the applicable rate, plus surcharge and cess, from each payment, including advances.
  • Deposit the tax and file the TDS statement for payments to non-residents. From 1 October 2026, a buyer who is a resident individual or HUF can deduct and deposit this TDS with a PAN-based challan-cum-statement, without a TAN. Companies and firms still need a TAN.
  • Issue a TDS certificate to the seller, who uses it to claim credit in the Indian return.

To estimate the TDS for a sale price, use the TDS on NRI property sale calculator.

The rates

Property held for more than 24 months gives a long-term capital gain, taxed at 12.5 per cent without indexation for non-residents. Property held for 24 months or less gives a short-term gain taxed at slab rates, and the buyer usually deducts at 30 per cent. Surcharge is 10 per cent where the amount exceeds ₹50 lakh and 15 per cent where it exceeds ₹1 crore; surcharge on these capital gains is capped at 15 per cent. Health and education cess of 4 per cent is added. On a long-term sale above ₹1 crore, TDS is therefore about 14.95 per cent of the sale price.

Worked example

Rahul, who lives in Singapore, sells a Borivali flat bought in 2012 for ₹60 lakh. The sale price is ₹1.5 crore.

Sale price1,50,00,000
Cost of acquisition60,00,000
Long-term capital gain90,00,000
Tax on the gain at 12.5% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess12,87,000
TDS on the full price at 12.5% + 15% surcharge + 4% cess22,42,500
Excess deducted, recoverable only as a refund9,55,500

Without a certificate, about ₹9.6 lakh stays with the tax department until Rahul files his return and the refund is processed. If he reinvests the gain to claim an exemption, the excess is larger still.

The lower deduction certificate

The seller can apply to the Assessing Officer, through the TRACES portal, for a certificate in Form 128 (formerly Form 13) allowing the buyer to deduct at a lower or nil rate based on the estimated actual tax. The application is supported by the purchase deed, the sale agreement, a computation of the gain and details of any planned exemption. The certificate is specific to the buyer and the transaction and takes time to issue, so it should be applied for as soon as the sale terms are agreed.

Exemptions that reduce the tax

  • Reinvestment of the long-term gain in a residential house in India, within the prescribed period and subject to the overall cap.
  • Investment of up to ₹50 lakh of the gain in specified bonds within six months of the transfer.

Where an exemption is planned, include it in the lower deduction application; otherwise TDS is deducted on a gain that ends up exempt.

After the sale

The seller files an Indian return for the tax year, reporting the gain, claiming the TDS credit and any exemption, and receiving a refund of any excess. Proceeds credited to an NRO account can be remitted abroad up to USD 1 million a financial year, with Form 145 (formerly Form 15CA) and, above the specified limits, a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146 (formerly Form 15CB).

Questions on TDS when an NRI sells property

How much TDS should a buyer deduct when buying property from an NRI?

The buyer deducts tax at the rate on the seller's capital gain, on the full price: 12.5% for property held over 24 months, or usually 30% if held for 24 months or less, plus surcharge (10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore) and 4% cess. On a long-term sale above ₹1 crore this is about 14.95% of the price, unless the seller has a lower deduction certificate.

Is TDS on an NRI property sale deducted on the full price or only on the gain?

On the full price. That is why TDS is often far higher than the actual tax. The seller can limit it to the tax on the gain by obtaining a lower deduction certificate in Form 128 before the sale.

How can an NRI reduce TDS on a property sale?

By applying on the TRACES portal for a lower deduction certificate in Form 128 (formerly Form 13), with the purchase deed, sale agreement, computation of the gain and any planned exemption. The buyer then deducts at the rate in the certificate. Apply as soon as the sale terms are agreed, because the certificate takes time to issue.

Does the buyer need a TAN to deduct TDS from an NRI seller?

From 1 October 2026, a buyer who is a resident individual or HUF can deduct and deposit this TDS using a PAN-based challan-cum-statement, without a TAN. Companies and firms buying from an NRI still need a TAN.

How does an NRI get back excess TDS on a property sale?

By filing an Indian income tax return for the tax year of the sale, reporting the gain, claiming the TDS credit and any exemption. The excess is refunded after the return is processed.

Can an NRI save capital gains tax by reinvesting the sale proceeds?

Yes. A long-term gain can be exempt if reinvested in a residential house in India within the prescribed period, subject to the overall cap, or if up to ₹50 lakh is invested in specified bonds within six months of the sale. Include the planned exemption in the lower deduction application.

How can an NRI send property sale proceeds abroad?

Proceeds credited to an NRO account can be remitted up to USD 1 million a financial year. The bank will ask for Form 145 (formerly Form 15CA) and, above the specified limits, a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146 (formerly Form 15CB).

Is TDS on the sale of a flat by an NRI different from a resident seller?

Yes. For a resident seller, TDS is 1% and only where the price is ₹50 lakh or more. For a non-resident seller there is no threshold, and TDS is at the capital gains rate on the full price, plus surcharge and cess.
