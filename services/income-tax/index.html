Income tax services under the Income-tax Act

Income tax work for individuals, professionals, HUFs, firms and companies: returns, planning, TDS compliance, reconciliation with the department's data, and representation when notices arrive. Handled from Borivali West, Mumbai, and remotely across India and abroad.

Compare the old and new regimes, or work out advance tax instalments, with the tax calculators.

Scope

What the work covers

Income tax is governed by its own law, now the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026, and its own portal, deadlines and procedures, separate from GST. A good return starts long before the filing date: with the right regime choice, advance tax paid on time, TDS credits that agree with Form 26AS, and income that agrees with the Annual Information Statement.

The practice handles both routine compliance and the questions that need more thought, such as capital gains on property and shares, income from abroad, business income for professionals and traders, and replies to notices.

Approach

How the work is done

Collect and check

Form 16, bank and broker statements, AIS and Form 26AS are collected and checked against each other before any figure goes into the return.

Compute and compare

Income is computed under both regimes where the choice is available, and the better position is explained in plain terms.

File and track

The return is filed and verified, and its processing is followed until the intimation is received and any refund is credited.

Respond when needed

Notices are answered point by point with a reconciliation and documents, within the time allowed.

Questions about income tax services

Which ITR form should I file?

It depends on the sources of income. Salaried individuals with simple income usually file ITR-1 or ITR-2; those with business or professional income file ITR-3, or ITR-4 under presumptive taxation; firms and LLPs file ITR-5; and companies file ITR-6. Capital gains, foreign assets or more than one house property also affect the choice.

Should I choose the old or the new tax regime?

The new regime has lower rates but allows few deductions; the old regime has higher rates but allows deductions such as for investments, insurance, home loan interest and rent. The better choice depends on your income and deductions, and should be compared each year.

What is the due date for filing my income tax return?

For FY 2025-26, the due date is 31 July 2026 for most individuals without business income, 31 August 2026 for business and professional taxpayers not subject to audit, and 21 November 2026 for audit cases (extended from 31 October by CBDT; the tax audit report is due 21 October 2026). See the compliance calendar for current dates.

I received an income tax notice. What should I do?

Read which section and year it relates to and the response date, then reconcile the items it raises with your records. Many notices arise from AIS mismatches and can be answered with a clear reconciliation. Do not ignore the deadline; an extension can be requested if more time is genuinely needed.

What are the major changes introduced by the Income-tax Act, 2025?

The Act replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. It uses simpler language, tables in place of long provisos, and fewer sections, and it replaces previous year and assessment year with tax year. TDS provisions are consolidated in section 393. It is mainly a rewrite rather than a change in tax policy, though later Finance Acts amend it.

What is the difference between the Income-tax Act, 1961 and the Income-tax Act, 2025?

The 1961 Act governs tax years beginning before 1 April 2026, and the 2025 Act governs later ones. The structure, section numbers, terms and forms differ, but most concepts, such as heads of income, residence and capital gains, carry over. Check the old and new text together on any point that matters, because a changed word can change the result.

How are the provisions of the old Act mapped to the new Act?

The Income Tax Department has published correlation tables. Some examples: section 139 is now section 263, section 148 is section 280, section 154 is section 287, section 90 is section 159, section 54 is section 82, and sections 194C, 194J and others are in section 393(1). Section 6 on residence keeps its number. Use the official tables rather than guessing, as one old section can split into several.

What is the concept of Tax Year under the new Act?

A tax year is the 12 months from 1 April to 31 March in which the income is earned. For a new business or a new source of income, it begins on the date of set-up or when the source arises, and ends on the next 31 March. The tax is assessed and the return is filed by reference to that same year.

What are the tax implications under the old and new tax regimes?

The new regime has lower slab rates and a Rs 60,000 rebate up to Rs 12 lakh of income, but allows few deductions. The old regime has higher rates, with deductions such as those formerly under sections 80C and 80D, HRA and home loan interest. The new regime applies unless you opt out. Special-rate income, such as capital gains on shares, is taxed the same way in both.

When should an individual opt for the old tax regime instead of the new regime?

When your deductions and exemptions are large enough to bring the old regime's tax below the new regime's. This usually needs a combination such as HRA, section 80C investments, health insurance and home loan interest. Work out both every year with your actual figures. You can try the regime calculator.

What deductions are available under the new tax regime?

The main ones are the Rs 75,000 standard deduction for salary and pension, the employer's NPS contribution up to 14% of basic and DA, a family pension deduction up to Rs 25,000, and the Agniveer Corpus Fund. Retirement exemptions, such as gratuity, also stay. Interest on a let-out property reduces its rent, but a loss cannot be set against other income.

How is the tax rebate calculated for an individual taxpayer?

A resident individual gets a rebate up to Rs 60,000 under the new regime if total income does not exceed Rs 12 lakh, and up to Rs 12,500 under the old regime if it does not exceed Rs 5 lakh. It reduces tax before cess (section 156, formerly section 87A). It does not apply against tax on income at special rates, such as gains on listed shares. Non-residents do not get it.
