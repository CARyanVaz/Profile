GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26: dates, limits and checks

Who has to file the GST annual return, the dates that matter, and what to reconcile first, since GSTR-9 cannot be revised after filing. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Status as on 5 November 2026.

Before filing

What to reconcile before filing

  • Books against GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, month by month, for sales and tax liability
  • Purchase register against GSTR-2B and IMS actions, with ITC claimed in GSTR-3B
  • Credit notes, debit notes and amendments, including those reported in the following year
  • ITC reversals and reclaims under rules 37, 37A, 42 and 43, and blocked credits under section 17(5)
  • HSN and rate-wise summaries, since FY 2025-26 covers the rate changes from 22 September 2025
  • Exports, refunds, RCM and any tax paid through DRC-03 during the year

Questions about GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C

What is the due date for GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26?

The due date for GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26 is 31 December 2026, unless the Government extends it. Filing opened on the GST portal in October 2026. All GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns for the year must be filed first.

Is GSTR-9 mandatory for turnover under Rs 2 crore?

No. Notification 15/2025-Central Tax dated 17 September 2025 exempts registered persons with aggregate turnover up to Rs 2 crore. Above that, GSTR-9 is required, except for classes outside section 44 such as input service distributors, tax deductors and collectors, casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons.

Who has to file GSTR-9C?

GSTR-9C is required where aggregate turnover for the year exceeds Rs 5 crore. It is a self-certified statement reconciling the annual return with the audited financial statements, and certification by a chartered accountant has not been required since the 2021 change to section 44.

What is the last date to claim ITC for FY 2025-26?

Under section 16(4), ITC for FY 2025-26 can be claimed only up to 30 November 2026 or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier. If you file GSTR-9 before 30 November, the window closes on that date. Complete the GSTR-2B reconciliation and claim what is due in the October 2026 GSTR-3B before then.

Can I claim missed ITC through GSTR-9?

No. GSTR-9 is a statement of what was reported during the year and cannot be used to take new credit. Missed ITC must be claimed in GSTR-3B within the section 16(4) time limit. If the annual return shows that tax was underpaid, pay it through DRC-03.

Can GSTR-9 be revised after filing?

No. There is no facility to revise GSTR-9 after it is filed, so reconcile before filing. Differences found later are generally dealt with through DRC-03 for extra tax, and they may be raised on scrutiny or by notice.

What is the late fee for GSTR-9?

Under Notification 07/2023-Central Tax, the total CGST and SGST fee is Rs 50 per day for turnover up to Rs 5 crore, Rs 100 per day from Rs 5 crore to Rs 20 crore, and Rs 200 per day above Rs 20 crore, subject to caps linked to turnover in the State or Union territory. There is also a risk of notice under section 46 and best judgement assessment if the return is not filed.

Why does GSTR-9 lead to GST notices?

Officers compare GSTR-9 with GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B and e-way bill data. Differences left unexplained often lead to ASMT-10 or a show cause notice, and the annual return due date also starts the time limit for those notices. See GST notices and how to reply.

What is the revised GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C procedure for FY 2025-26?

Filing is on the GST portal in the usual way, once all GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns for the year are filed. Notification 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026 extends the simplified reporting options, such as combining certain tables, to FY 2025-26 onwards. Read the current instructions on the portal for each table before filing.

What records do I need for GSTR-9?

Keep GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B for April 2025 to March 2026, and the amendments reported up to the cut-off in the following year. You also need sales and purchase registers, debit and credit notes, ITC reversal workings, HSN-wise summaries, DRC-03 challans, and audited or draft financial statements.
