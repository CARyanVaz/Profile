Scope

What the work covers

A non-resident is taxed in India only on income that arises or is received in India. In practice, most NRIs still have Indian income: interest on NRO deposits, rent from a flat in Mumbai, dividends, mutual fund redemptions or the sale of a property. Each of these carries its own withholding rate, reporting and repatriation rules.

From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, and the familiar 'previous year' and 'assessment year' are replaced by a single 'tax year'. The substance of NRI taxation is largely carried forward, but section and form numbers have changed, so older guidance found online needs to be read with care.

  • Tax on NRO interest, rent, dividends and capital gains, and the exemption for NRE deposit interest
  • Sale of property in India by a non-resident: computation of gains, TDS by the buyer, and lower or nil deduction certificates
  • Relief under tax treaties (DTAA), with Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F)

Approach

How the work is done

Establish status

Days of stay in India for the year and the preceding years are counted from passport records, and Indian income is totalled to apply the stay and deemed-residency tests.

Map every income source

Bank, broker, mutual fund and property records are matched against the Annual Information Statement so that nothing reported by third parties is missed.

Plan withholding before it happens

For a property sale, the expected gain is computed early. Where TDS on the full sale value would far exceed the actual tax, an application for a lower deduction certificate is made before the sale deed.

File and repatriate

The return is filed with treaty claims where applicable, refunds are tracked, and Forms 145 and 146 (formerly 15CA and 15CB) are prepared for moving funds abroad.

Why residential status matters

Whether you are taxed in India on worldwide income or only on Indian income depends on your residential status for the tax year. It is decided by days of stay, not by passport, visa or the type of bank account you hold. The rules are in section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which keeps the same section number as the 1961 Act. From 1 April 2026 the period is called the tax year (1 April to 31 March).

Residential status for income tax is separate from status under foreign exchange law (FEMA), which governs bank accounts and investments. A person can be non-resident for FEMA and resident for income tax, or the other way round, so both should be checked. For bank accounts after returning, see NRE, NRO and RFC accounts after returning to India.

The basic tests

An individual is resident in India for a tax year if either of the following is true:

  • they were in India for 182 days or more during the year, or
  • they were in India for 60 days or more during the year and 365 days or more in total during the four preceding years.

Anyone who meets neither test is non-resident. Days are counted from passport stamps or travel records; the day of arrival and the day of departure are usually both counted as days in India.

Exceptions to the 60-day test

The 60-day test does not apply, and 182 days is used instead, for two groups:

  • Indian citizens who leave India during the year for employment abroad, or as crew of an Indian ship
  • Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin who live abroad and come on a visit to India, if their Indian income (excluding income from foreign sources) is ₹15 lakh or less.

If a visiting Indian citizen or person of Indian origin has Indian income above ₹15 lakh, the 60 days becomes 120 days. They are resident if they stay 120 days or more in the year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years. Someone who becomes resident only through this 120-day rule is treated as Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).

Deemed residency

An Indian citizen whose Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, and who is not liable to tax in any other country because of domicile or residence, is deemed to be resident in India whatever the number of days. A deemed resident is treated as RNOR.

RNOR: the status that matters for returning Indians

A resident is either ordinarily resident or not ordinarily resident (RNOR). An RNOR is taxed in India on Indian income, and on foreign income only if it comes from a business controlled in, or a profession set up in, India. Other foreign income, such as salary earned abroad before return, interest on foreign deposits and foreign rent, is not taxed in India in RNOR years.

A resident individual is RNOR if either of the following applies:

  • they were non-resident in nine out of the ten preceding years, or
  • they were in India for 729 days or less in total during the seven preceding years.

For someone returning after a decade abroad, this usually means one to three years of RNOR status, which is time to reorganise foreign assets and accounts before full residency begins.

What depends on the status

Status decides what income is taxed, which return form and schedules are used, whether foreign assets and foreign income must be reported, and whether treaty relief is available. A missed residency can mean undeclared foreign income; an assumed residency can mean paying Indian tax on income that was not taxable here. For anyone near the thresholds, a day count kept through the year is the simplest protection. More questions are answered on NRI tax questions.

Yes. Documents are shared by email or a shared drive, the AIS and Form 26AS are downloaded with the NRI's login or as an authorised representative, and returns are verified electronically. Meetings are held by video call at a time that suits the NRI's time zone.

Is interest on NRE deposits taxable?

Interest on NRE deposits is exempt only while the holder is a person resident outside India under FEMA. The exemption ends when the holder becomes a FEMA resident, even if still RNOR for income tax. NRO interest is taxable in India and is subject to TDS at a higher rate than for residents, though treaty rates may apply with the right documents.

How much TDS does a buyer deduct when an NRI sells property?

The buyer is required to deduct tax on the sale consideration at the rate applicable to the non-resident's capital gain, plus surcharge and cess, unless the seller obtains a lower or nil deduction certificate. Because the deduction is on the sale value, not the gain, the certificate often makes a large difference to cash received.

Can an NRI repatriate the proceeds of a property sale?

Funds in an NRO account, including sale proceeds, can be remitted abroad up to USD 1 million per financial year, subject to tax having been paid. The bank will usually ask for Form 145 (formerly Form 15CA) and, above the specified limits, a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146 (formerly Form 15CB).

How can an NRI sell a flat in Borivali, Kandivali or Malad from abroad?

Usually through a power of attorney to a trusted person in India, attested abroad as the sub-registrar requires and stamped in Maharashtra, or by visiting to sign. The buyer deducts TDS on the full price, unless you have a certificate in Form 128, so apply well before the sale. The proceeds go to an NRO account, and you file a return for that year.

Does an NRI need a Mumbai chartered accountant to sell property or file a return?

It is not compulsory to use one. You can file your own return online. A chartered accountant is usually needed for Form 146 (formerly Form 15CB) when remitting money abroad in many cases, and many sellers also take help with a capital gains working and a lower TDS application. Choose someone who can work online across your time zone.

What should an NRI do with rent from a flat in Mumbai?

The tenant generally deducts TDS at 30% plus surcharge and cess on rent paid to a non-resident, with no Rs 50,000 monthly limit. Rent should be credited to an NRO account. Report it in your Indian return, with the 30% standard deduction, municipal taxes and home loan interest as allowed. A lower deduction certificate can help where tax is much lower than TDS.

Can a family member in Mumbai help an NRI with Indian tax work?

Yes, in practical ways, such as collecting documents or signing under a valid power of attorney. The return is still yours, so verify it yourself where you can, or authorise a representative as the rules allow. Keep your portal login, email and mobile number under your control, because notices are often sent only there.

Who is an NRI under Indian income tax?

For income tax, a non-resident is anyone who is not resident in India in the tax year under the residence tests, which turn on days spent in India: 182 days or more in the year, or 60 days or more in the year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years, with the 60-day test replaced by 182 days for Indian citizens leaving for a job abroad and for visiting Indian citizens or persons of Indian origin with Indian income up to ₹15 lakh, and by 120 days where that Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh. Status is decided afresh each tax year. Under FEMA, which governs bank accounts and investments, the test is different: it looks at why you are outside India (employment, business or an intention to stay abroad), not the number of days.

How many days can an NRI stay in India without becoming resident?

For most visiting Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin whose Indian income is ₹15 lakh or less, the limit is under 182 days in the tax year. If Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, staying 120 days or more makes them resident if they also spent 365 days or more in India over the preceding four years.

What is the 120-day rule for NRIs?

It applies to Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin visiting India whose Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh. They become resident if they are in India for 120 days or more in the tax year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years. Anyone who is resident only because of this rule is treated as RNOR.

Who is a deemed resident of India?

An Indian citizen whose Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh and who is not liable to tax in any other country because of domicile or residence is deemed resident in India, whatever the number of days. A deemed resident is treated as Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident.

What is RNOR status and how long does it last?

RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) applies to a resident who was non-resident in nine of the ten preceding years, or was in India for 729 days or less in the seven preceding years. Foreign income, other than from a business controlled or profession set up in India, is not taxed in India in RNOR years. For someone returning after about ten years abroad, it usually lasts one to three years.

Is it mandatory for an NRI to file an income tax return in India?

It is required if total Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit, which for FY 2025-26 is Rs 4 lakh under the new regime. You must also file to claim a TDS refund, and usually after selling property. Where the only income is investment income or specified long-term gains with full tax deducted, filing may not be required (section 216, formerly section 115G).

How can an NRI file an income tax return from abroad?

Register on the income tax e-filing portal with your PAN, overseas mobile and email. Download AIS and Form 26AS, work out Indian income and TDS, select non-resident status and give the days spent in India. File online and verify the return. A pre-validated Indian bank account is needed for a refund.

Which ITR form should an NRI use?

Most NRIs use ITR-2, which covers salary, house property, capital gains and other sources. ITR-3 applies if there is business or professional income in India. ITR-1 and ITR-4 are for residents only. Filing the wrong form can lead to a defective return notice and delay any refund.

Can an NRI file an income tax return without visiting India?

Yes. Filing and verification are done online, and most notices are answered online too. You do not need to travel or give a power of attorney to file a return, though your Indian mobile or bank details make verification easier.

Can a Chartered Accountant file my Indian tax return while I am overseas?

Yes. A chartered accountant can prepare the computation from your documents and file it through the portal, with your authorisation. You still verify the return yourself, and you remain responsible for its contents.

What documents are required for NRI income tax return filing?

PAN and portal login, passport pages showing travel dates, AIS and Form 26AS, and TDS certificates. Add NRO and NRE statements with interest certificates, rent agreements, capital gains statements, purchase and sale deeds for property, and a Tax Residency Certificate with Form 41 if claiming treaty relief. Also keep details of an Indian bank account for the refund.

What is the due date for NRI income tax return filing?

For FY 2025-26, the due date was 31 July 2026 for most individuals without business income and 31 August 2026 for business cases not subject to audit. Audit cases have until 21 November 2026. A belated return can be filed up to 31 December 2026 with a late fee. See the compliance calendar for current dates.

Can an NRI claim a refund of excess TDS?

Yes, by filing the return for that year and showing the actual tax on your income. This is common on property sales, NRO interest and mutual fund gains, because TDS is often higher than the final tax. The refund goes to a pre-validated bank account in your name, so validate the account before filing.

How do I transfer money from an NRO account to an NRE account?

Transfers from NRO to NRE are allowed up to USD 1 million in a financial year, counted together with any other remittances from NRO balances, after the tax on the money has been paid. The bank usually asks for its transfer request form, a FEMA declaration confirming the USD 1 million limit, evidence of the source of funds, Form 145 (formerly Form 15CA) and a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146 (formerly Form 15CB).
