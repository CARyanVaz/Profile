NRI taxation and income tax for non-residents

Indian tax compliance for non-resident Indians and returning Indians, handled from Borivali West, Mumbai, and remotely for clients overseas. The work starts with residential status for the year and then applies the right treatment to each source of Indian income.

Buying property from an NRI? Work out the TDS with the NRI property TDS calculator.

Scope

What the work covers

A non-resident is taxed in India only on income that arises or is received in India. In practice, most NRIs still have Indian income: interest on NRO deposits, rent from a flat in Mumbai, dividends, mutual fund redemptions or the sale of a property. Each of these carries its own withholding rate, reporting and repatriation rules.

From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, and the familiar 'previous year' and 'assessment year' are replaced by a single 'tax year'. The substance of NRI taxation is largely carried forward, but section and form numbers have changed, so older guidance found online needs to be read with care.

  • Determining residential status for the year, including RNOR status for Indians returning after years abroad
  • Tax on NRO interest, rent, dividends and capital gains, and the exemption for NRE deposit interest
  • Sale of property in India by a non-resident: computation of gains, TDS by the buyer, and lower or nil deduction certificates
  • Relief under tax treaties (DTAA), with Tax Residency Certificate and Form 10F
  • Remittance documentation: Form 15CA and the chartered accountant's certificate in Form 15CB
  • Income tax return filing for non-residents and claims of excess TDS as refund
  • Replies to tax department notices and AIS mismatches for NRI taxpayers

Approach

How the work is done

Establish status

Days of stay in India for the year and the preceding years are counted from passport records, and Indian income is totalled to apply the stay and deemed-residency tests.

Map every income source

Bank, broker, mutual fund and property records are matched against the Annual Information Statement so that nothing reported by third parties is missed.

Plan withholding before it happens

For a property sale, the expected gain is computed early. Where TDS on the full sale value would far exceed the actual tax, an application for a lower deduction certificate is made before the sale deed.

File and repatriate

The return is filed with treaty claims where applicable, refunds are tracked, and Form 15CA/15CB documentation is prepared for moving funds abroad.

Getting started

Records to keep ready

  • Passport pages with entry and exit stamps, or travel history
  • NRE and NRO bank statements and interest certificates
  • Purchase and sale deeds, and cost of improvement records, for any property
  • Tax Residency Certificate from the country of residence, where treaty relief is claimed
  • Login access to, or downloads of, AIS and Form 26AS

Returning to India: NRE, NRO and RFC accounts

Your bank accounts follow FEMA, not the 182-day income tax test. If you return to work, run a business or settle in India, your NRE and NRO accounts must be redesignated from the day you arrive.

What happens to an NRE account after returning to India?

If you return to work, run a business or settle in India, you become a person resident in India under FEMA from the day you arrive, and the NRE account must be redesignated. The balance moves to a resident savings account or, at your option, to a Resident Foreign Currency (RFC) account. A visit for a holiday, a wedding or medical treatment does not trigger this.

Can I keep my NRE account after returning to India?

No, not after you become a FEMA resident. The test is why you are in India, not the 182-day income tax test, so being a non-resident for income tax does not let you keep the account. Holding it after that date is a contravention of the FEMA deposit regulations, and interest treated as exempt may have to be offered to tax.

How do I convert an NRE or NRO account to a resident account?

Tell each bank in writing soon after you arrive, using its change of residential status form, and give your date of return. Banks usually ask for your passport with the arrival stamp, PAN, Aadhaar, proof of Indian address and a FATCA/CRS declaration. Most banks keep the same account number, but confirm in writing that your debit card, UPI and mandates continue. NRO balances can go only to a resident account, not to RFC.

How do I open a resident account after returning as an NRI?

You usually do not need a new account. The bank redesignates the existing NRE or NRO account as a resident account once you submit its status change form. If you want to keep foreign currency, open an RFC account at the same time and instruct the bank to move the NRE balance into it.

How do I close an NRE account after returning to India?

Ask the bank to redesignate the account or transfer the balance, rather than simply leaving it unused. If you do not want to continue with that bank, give written instructions to move the balance to your resident or RFC account and then close it. Close or update linked items as well, including PIS and NRE/NRO demat accounts, mutual fund folios and KYC records.

Can an NRI continue a fixed deposit after returning to India?

Yes. An NRE or NRO fixed deposit is redesignated as a resident deposit and can usually run to maturity at the contracted rate, but interest accruing after you become a FEMA resident is taxable. An FCNR(B) deposit can be held until maturity, and its interest stays exempt while you are RNOR. Check premature withdrawal terms before breaking an NRE deposit to move it to RFC.

Is RFC better than a resident account for a returning NRI?

Often, if you want to keep savings in foreign currency. RFC balances can be remitted abroad for any bona fide purpose, and RFC interest is exempt while you are RNOR. It becomes taxable once you are resident and ordinarily resident. RFC suits money you may need abroad; a resident account is simpler for money you will spend in India.

Who is a resident of India?

There are two separate tests. Under FEMA, you are resident from the day you come to India for employment, business or an uncertain period of stay, and this governs your bank accounts. Under income tax, residence depends on the number of days spent in India, and it governs how your income is taxed. You can be a FEMA resident while still a non-resident or RNOR for income tax.

What happens if an NRI account becomes dormant?

Under RBI rules, a savings or current account is treated as inoperative if there are no transactions for over two years. You can usually reactivate it by contacting the bank with a written request, proof of identity and updated KYC. Balances unclaimed for over 10 years may be transferred to RBI's Depositor Education and Awareness Fund, and can still be claimed through the bank.

Questions about NRI taxation

Does an NRI need to file an income tax return in India?

An NRI must file a return if total Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit. Filing is also the only way to claim a refund where TDS deducted (for example on NRO interest or a property sale) is more than the tax actually payable, so many NRIs file even when not strictly required to.

Is interest on NRE deposits taxable?

Interest on NRE deposits is exempt only while the holder is a person resident outside India under FEMA. The exemption ends when the holder becomes a FEMA resident, even if still RNOR for income tax. NRO interest is taxable in India and is subject to TDS at a higher rate than for residents, though treaty rates may apply with the right documents.

How much TDS does a buyer deduct when an NRI sells property?

The buyer is required to deduct tax on the sale consideration at the rate applicable to the non-resident's capital gain, plus surcharge and cess, unless the seller obtains a lower or nil deduction certificate. Because the deduction is on the sale value, not the gain, the certificate often makes a large difference to cash received.

What is RNOR status?

Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident is a transitional status, usually available to Indians returning after a long period abroad. During RNOR years, foreign income is generally not taxed in India unless it is derived from a business controlled or a profession set up in India.

Can an NRI repatriate the proceeds of a property sale?

Funds in an NRO account, including sale proceeds, can be remitted abroad up to USD 1 million per financial year, subject to tax having been paid. The bank will usually ask for Form 15CA and, above the specified limits, a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 15CB.
