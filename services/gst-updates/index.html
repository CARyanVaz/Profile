GST updates: changes from October 2026 to January 2027

What changed, from when, and what is still only recommended, with answers to the questions these changes raise. Compiled by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai. General information: check the notification before relying on it.

Status as on 5 November 2026. Changes in income tax and other laws are listed in News.

Questions on the GST changes

What are the new GST registration rules effective from 1 November 2026?

Notification 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026 revises Form REG-01 and rule 19 on amendments, adds rule 14B for e-commerce sellers, and substitutes rules 20 and 21 on cancellation. Circular 257/03/2026-GST gives officers document lists and limits on queries. Registration is generally processed in 7 working days with Aadhaar authentication and 30 days otherwise, and is deemed approved under rule 9 if the time is missed. Revised suspension and cancellation procedures follow from 1 April 2027.

What are the changes in GST REG-01 from November 2026?

Form REG-01 is substituted from 1 November 2026. It now has options for registration under rules 14A and 14B, fields for the e-commerce operator's warehouse, a nature of business for supplies made only through an operator, and drop-down document lists. A new Form REG-05A intimates a deemed rejection where the applicant does not respond to a notice or verification.

What is the new Rule 14B simplified GST registration scheme?

Under rule 14B, inserted by Notification 05/2026-Central Tax from 1 November 2026, a person registered in one State who supplies goods through an e-commerce operator can register in a State with no physical presence, declaring the operator's warehouse as the principal place of business. Output tax on supplies to registered persons, excluding those on the same PAN, must not exceed Rs 2.5 lakh a month. The operator must consent within 7 working days, Aadhaar authentication is required, and the registration covers supplies through that platform only.

Can e-commerce sellers obtain GST registration without a physical presence in the destination state?

Yes, under rule 14B from 1 November 2026, if goods are supplied through an e-commerce operator whose warehouse in that State is declared as the principal place of business, the seller is registered in a home State and the Rs 2.5 lakh monthly limit on output tax on supplies to registered persons is met. The application is made in Form REG-01 with the operator's consent. See the full answer.

What is the new automatic GST registration cancellation process under REG-16?

From 1 November 2026, under rule 20 and a GSTN advisory, a compliant REG-16 application can be approved by the system. All returns due must be filed. Composition taxpayers, non-resident and OIDAR taxpayers and ISDs are cancelled electronically once returns are filed. Regular, casual and SEZ taxpayers are too, where output tax is up to Rs 2.5 lakh in every period. Above that, GSTR-10 must be filed within 3 days of the stated date, or the application goes to the officer. A revised REG-16 applies from 1 December 2026.

What are the changes in GST registration amendment under REG-14?

From 1 November 2026, Form REG-14 and rule 19 are substituted. Amendments are to be applied for within 15 days of the change, and most take effect automatically on filing. A change of principal place of business goes to the officer, with automatic amendment for rule 14A registrants from 1 December 2026. Composition taxpayers can also update their category through REG-14, across all GSTINs on the same PAN.

What are the new GST TDS provisions applicable to scrap dealers?

From 1 November 2026, Notification 07/2026-Central Tax requires a registered buyer to deduct GST TDS under section 51 on purchases of the listed goods from registered suppliers, where the contract value exceeds Rs 2.5 lakh. The buyer needs separate TDS registration in Form REG-07 and files GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month. Notification 06/2026-Central Tax also withdraws the registration exemption for suppliers of these goods.

Is GST TDS applicable to used cooking oil, plastic scrap, tyre scrap and e-waste?

Yes, from 1 November 2026, when bought by a registered person from a registered supplier. The goods are used cooking oil (heading 1518), waste and scrap of plastics (3915), waste and scrap of tyres (4004), metal scrap (Chapters 72 to 81, already covered) and electrical and electronic waste (8549). If the supplier is unregistered, TDS does not apply, and the buyer pays tax under reverse charge instead (Notification 04/2026-Central Tax (Rate)).

What is the 2% GST TDS requirement on scrap purchases above Rs 2.5 lakh?

The buyer deducts 2% of the taxable value, excluding GST: 1% CGST and 1% SGST on supplies within a State, or 2% IGST on inter-State supplies. It applies only where the total value of supply under one contract exceeds Rs 2.5 lakh. The amount is deposited and reported in GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month.

What are the new GST composition scheme categories from November 2026?

Under Notification 05/2026-Central Tax, Table 5 of Forms CMP-02 and CMP-04 now lists manufacturers (other than of notified goods), restaurant and catering suppliers under paragraph 6(b) of Schedule II, other persons eligible under section 10(1), and persons eligible under section 10(2A). The section 10(2A) category cannot be selected together with the section 10(1) categories. Forms CMP-08 and GSTR-4 also change from 1 November 2026, to report goods, services and section 9(5) supplies separately.

What are the new GST rules for post-sale discounts from 1 November?

From 1 November 2026, under Notification 03/2026-Central Tax, section 15(3)(b) is substituted. A discount given after supply is excluded from taxable value if the supplier issues a credit note under section 34 and the recipient reverses the related ITC. Section 34 now lists post-sale discount as a ground for a credit note. The earlier conditions on an agreement made at or before the supply no longer apply.

Can a supplier issue a GST credit note without a prior agreement for post-sale discounts?

Yes, from 1 November 2026. A prior agreement is no longer required for the discount to reduce taxable value. The supplier cannot reduce output tax, however, if a registered recipient has not reversed the ITC on the credit note, or where the tax burden has been passed on to another person. The time limit in section 34(2) continues to apply. Check supplies made before 1 November 2026 separately.

What are the new GST refund rules for inverted duty structure?

Under rule 89(5), as substituted by Notification 05/2026-Central Tax, ITC on input services availed on or after 1 November 2026 is included in inverted duty refunds. ITC on capital goods availed on or after 1 April 2027 is refundable over 60 months. Earlier credit is not covered. See the full answer.

Can 90% of an inverted duty structure refund be sanctioned provisionally?

Yes. From 1 November 2026, under section 54(6) as amended and the rules, 90% of an inverted duty or zero-rated refund can be sanctioned provisionally through the portal on a risk-based check, within 3 working days of acknowledgement. The changes are being phased in as the system becomes ready, and the revised Form RFD-01 applies from 1 December 2026.

What is the new 10-day GST refund acknowledgement requirement?

Under rules 90 and 91 as amended from 1 November 2026, the department must issue an acknowledgement or a deficiency memo within 10 days instead of 15, failing which the application is treated as acknowledged. This applies to new claims. Claims already pending follow the existing process.

What is the revised GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C filing procedure for FY 2025-26?

The simplified reporting options are extended to FY 2025-26 onwards by Notification 05/2026-Central Tax. All GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns must be filed first, and the due date is 31 December 2026. See GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26.

What are the GST return filing due dates for November 2026?

For the October 2026 period, GSTR-7 and GSTR-8 are due on 10 November, GSTR-1 on 11 November, IFF and GSTR-6 on 13 November, GSTR-3B for monthly filers on 20 November, and PMT-06 for QRMP taxpayers on 25 November 2026. The November 2026 period falls due on the same days of December.

What is the proposed 14-digit E-Way Bill number?

A GSTN advisory dated 4 November 2026 says e-way bill numbers will go from 12 to 14 digits, and consolidated e-way bill numbers from 10 to 11 digits, as the present numbers are running out. Businesses using ERP systems or APIs should update fields and validation rules to accept the longer numbers.

When will the new E-Way Bill numbering system be implemented?

From 1 January 2027, according to the GSTN advisory of 4 November 2026. The changes are to appear in the sandbox first, so that software can be tested, and further technical details are to be published on the e-way bill portal.

What are the GST changes applicable from 1 November 2026 and 1 January 2027?

From 1 November 2026: new registration forms and rules, rule 14B, post-sale discounts, refund changes, TDS on specified scrap and revised composition forms. From 1 January 2027: the new e-way bill numbering, e-invoice schema with codes for reverse charge self-invoices (Notifications 04/2026 and 05/2026-Central Tax), and revised GSTR-7A. Between them, revised REG-16 and RFD-01 apply from 1 December 2026.
