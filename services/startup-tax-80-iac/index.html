Section 80-IAC: the startup tax holiday, now section 140

How a startup claims the three-year deduction, and what DPIIT recognition does not cover. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Questions on section 80-IAC

What is the section 80-IAC tax holiday?

It is a 100% deduction of profits from an eligible startup business, for any three consecutive years within the first ten years from incorporation. For tax year 2026-27 onwards, the provision is section 140 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, on broadly the same conditions. For FY 2025-26 and earlier, section 80-IAC of the 1961 Act applies.

Who is eligible for the section 80-IAC deduction?

A private limited company or LLP, incorporated from 1 April 2016 and before 1 April 2030, with DPIIT recognition and a certificate from the Inter-Ministerial Board. Turnover must not exceed Rs 100 crore in the year of claim. Partnership firms, one-person companies and proprietorships cannot claim it, and the business must not be formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing one.

Is DPIIT recognition enough to claim 80-IAC?

No. DPIIT recognition is free and quick, and it is required first, but it does not give the deduction. You also need a separate certificate of eligibility from the Inter-Ministerial Board, which reviews innovation, scalability, market potential and employment. Fewer startups clear this stage, so a well-supported application matters.

What is the turnover limit for 80-IAC after the 2026 DPIIT notification?

The DPIIT notification dated 4 February 2026 raised the recognition limit to Rs 200 crore, and Rs 300 crore for deep tech startups. Commentators say the 80-IAC limit stays at Rs 100 crore in the year of claim, as no change has been reported. So a startup can be recognised and still not qualify for the deduction in a year with higher turnover.

How do I apply to the Inter-Ministerial Board for 80-IAC?

Apply online through the Startup India portal after DPIIT recognition. Set out the product or process, what is innovative, your business model and how it scales, supported by financial statements and any patents or other evidence. One source reports review within about 120 days of a complete application. Check the current portal checklist before filing.

Which three years should a startup choose for 80-IAC?

Generally the three consecutive years with the highest expected profits, within the ten-year window and while turnover is under the limit. Starting in a loss year wastes part of the benefit. Project profits, turnover and MAT before choosing. Once started, the years run consecutively.

Does MAT apply to a startup claiming 80-IAC?

Yes, for a company that is under the normal regime: Minimum Alternate Tax on book profits can still apply in the holiday years, and generally gives a credit under the rules. LLPs have their own alternate minimum tax rules. Budget 2026 changed parts of the MAT regime, so work out the current position before relying on this benefit.

Can a company claim 80-IAC and the 22% concessional tax rate?

Generally not. A company opting for the concessional rate formerly in section 115BAA gives up most Chapter VI-A deductions, including this one. Compare both for your expected profits and MAT position. A company that does not expect taxable profits in the window may find the concessional route simpler.

What documents and filings are needed to claim 80-IAC?

Keep the DPIIT recognition, Inter-Ministerial Board certificate, audited accounts, the prescribed audit report, and a working that separates profit from the eligible business. Claim the deduction in a return filed by the due date, because a late return generally loses it. Keep a yearly schedule of turnover against the Rs 100 crore limit.

What is the difference between 80-IAC, the angel tax and carry forward of losses for startups?

80-IAC is a deduction on profits. Angel tax on share premium, formerly section 56(2)(viib), was abolished from FY 2024-25, so it no longer needs an exemption for new issues. Separately, an eligible startup can carry forward losses despite a change in shareholding, if conditions are met. Each has its own tests, so check them separately.
