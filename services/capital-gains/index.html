Capital gains tax: rates, exemptions and common transactions

How gains on property, shares and funds are worked out for tax year 2026-27, and which exemptions are available. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Status as on 5 November 2026.

Questions on capital gains

How are short-term and long-term capital gains computed?

Start with the sale price, deduct transfer costs, then cost of acquisition and improvement. Listed shares and equity funds are long-term after 12 months, and property and most unlisted assets after 24 months. Indexation is generally not available on current long-term sales, with one exception for resident owners of older land and buildings. Holding period runs from the date of acquisition.

What are the applicable capital gains tax rates?

Listed equity shares and equity funds on which STT is paid: 20% short-term, and 12.5% long-term on gains above Rs 1.25 lakh a year. Other long-term assets: generally 12.5%. Other short-term assets: slab rates. Debt funds bought from 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rates. Surcharge and 4% cess are added. See the table on this page.

What is the tax treatment of sale of residential property?

Held over 24 months, the gain is long-term, taxed at 12.5% without indexation. A resident individual or HUF who bought before 23 July 2024 can choose 20% with indexation if that is lower. Held for less, it is added to income at slab rates. If stamp duty value exceeds the price by over 10%, the stamp duty value is generally used. The buyer may deduct 1% TDS.

What are the exemptions available under sections 54, 54F and 54EC?

Section 82 (formerly 54): gain on a house, reinvested in a house in India. Section 86 (formerly 54F): sale of another long-term asset, with the net sale proceeds reinvested in a house. Both allow purchase one year before or two years after sale, or building within three years, capped at Rs 10 crore. Section 85 (formerly 54EC): up to Rs 50 lakh in specified bonds within six months, for land or buildings.

What is the tax treatment of compulsory acquisition of property?

The gain is taxed in the year the compensation is first received, and any later enhanced compensation is taxed when received. Compensation for urban agricultural land, and acquisitions under the 2013 land acquisition law, can be exempt if conditions are met. Read the award and the law it is under before filing, as the treatment depends on these.

How is the cost of acquisition determined for inherited property?

Cost is what the previous owner paid, and the previous owner's holding period counts. For property acquired before 1 April 2001, you can use its fair market value on that date, but not above stamp duty value for land and buildings. Keep the will or succession document, the old deed and any valuation report.

How are capital gains calculated on sale of unlisted shares?

Sale price less cost and transfer costs. Held over 24 months, the gain is long-term, taxed at 12.5%; otherwise, it is taxed at slab rates. If the price is below fair market value under the rules, that value can be taken as the sale price. Buyers paying below fair market value may also be taxed. Keep a valuation report.

What is the tax treatment of sale of listed equity shares?

Where STT is paid: gains held for 12 months or less are taxed at 20%. Longer-held gains are taxed at 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh a year in total. Shares bought before 1 February 2018 can use the 31 January 2018 price as cost, within limits. Report the sale in the capital gains schedule, and match it with AIS.

How are capital gains on mutual funds calculated?

Sale value less purchase cost, unit by unit, with the earliest units treated as sold first. Equity funds follow the rates for listed shares. Debt funds bought from 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rates however long they are held. Other funds depend on the type of fund and holding period. Use the capital gains statement from the fund or registrar.

What is the tax treatment of bonus shares and rights shares?

Bonus shares are not taxed on receipt. On sale, cost is generally nil and the holding period runs from allotment, except for the 31 January 2018 rule on older bonus shares. Rights shares have a cost equal to the amount paid. If you sell the right itself, the gain is short-term, with nil cost for the original holder.

How are capital gains calculated on transfer of depreciable assets?

Depreciable assets are in a block. Sale proceeds reduce the block, and a gain arises only if proceeds exceed the block value, or the block ceases to exist. The gain is treated as short-term, whatever the holding period. The 12.5% rate may still apply in some cases, as courts have held for long-held assets, so check this if the amount is material.

What is the tax treatment of gifts of immovable property and shares?

The giver has no capital gains on a gift. A receiver who is a relative pays no tax. A non-relative pays tax if property stamp duty value exceeds Rs 50,000, or if shares and other movable property exceed Rs 50,000 in total in the year at fair market value. On later sale, the receiver uses the previous owner's cost and holding period. See gifts.

What are the tax implications of joint development agreements?

For an individual or HUF landowner with a registered agreement, the gain is taxed in the year the completion certificate is issued, not when the land is handed over. The value is the stamp duty value of the owner's share on that date, plus any cash. The developer deducts 10% TDS on cash paid. Sale of the share before the certificate, or an unregistered agreement, changes the result.
