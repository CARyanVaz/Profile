Income-tax Act, 2025: what changed and how the transition works

What the new Act changes from 1 April 2026, and what stays under the 1961 Act. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Status as on 5 November 2026.

Questions on the Income-tax Act, 2025

What are the major changes introduced by the Income-tax Act, 2025?

The Act replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. It uses simpler language, tables in place of long provisos, and fewer sections, and it replaces previous year and assessment year with tax year. TDS provisions are consolidated in section 393. It is mainly a rewrite rather than a change in tax policy, though later Finance Acts amend it.

What is the difference between the Income-tax Act, 1961 and the Income-tax Act, 2025?

The 1961 Act governs tax years beginning before 1 April 2026, and the 2025 Act governs later ones. The structure, section numbers, terms and forms differ, but most concepts, such as heads of income, residence and capital gains, carry over. Check the old and new text together on any point that matters, because a changed word can change the result.

How are the provisions of the old Act mapped to the new Act?

The Income Tax Department has published correlation tables. Some examples: section 139 is now section 263, section 148 is section 280, section 154 is section 287, section 90 is section 159, section 54 is section 82, and sections 194C, 194J and others are in section 393(1). Section 6 on residence keeps its number. Use the official tables rather than guessing, as one old section can split into several.

What is the concept of Tax Year under the new Act?

A tax year is the 12 months from 1 April to 31 March in which the income is earned. For a new business or a new source of income, it begins on the date of set-up or when the source arises, and ends on the next 31 March. The tax is assessed and the return is filed by reference to that same year.

How are Assessment Year and Previous Year replaced?

Both are replaced by the single term tax year. Income of 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 is income of tax year 2026-27. Under the old Act, the same period would have been previous year 2026-27 and assessment year 2027-28. Earlier years keep the old terms.

Which Act applies to income earned up to 31 March 2026?

The Income-tax Act, 1961, through the savings provision in section 536 of the 2025 Act. So the return for FY 2025-26, its assessment, and any penalty or appeal for that year follow the 1961 Act, even though they take place after 1 April 2026.

How are pending assessments and appeals dealt with under the new Act?

CBDT's FAQs issued on 6 July 2026 say proceedings for tax years before 1 April 2026 continue under the 1961 Act, including those started later for such years. Assessments for tax year 2026-27 onwards fall under the 2025 Act. Appeals on earlier years should be approached in the same way, and the notice or order should show the provision being used.

What happens to existing registrations, approvals and exemptions?

They generally continue. CBDT's FAQs say section 12AB and 80G registrations already granted remain valid and protected under section 536. Applications filed by 31 March 2026 and still pending may be disposed of under the old Act or, for relief from tax year 2026-27, treated as filed under the new one. Check the expiry date on each approval, as renewal is still needed.

What are the changes in Income-tax Rules and prescribed forms?

The Income-tax Rules, 2026 apply from 1 April 2026, with renumbered forms. Examples: Form 16 is now 130, Form 16A is 131, Form 26Q is 140, Form 24Q is 138, Form 13 is 128, Forms 15G and 15H are 121, and Forms 3CA, 3CB and 3CD are 26. Old forms continue for old periods, so the form depends on the year and not the date of filing.

How are tax payments and advance tax obligations handled during the transition?

Advance tax for FY 2025-26, including the March 2026 instalment, fell under the 1961 Act. Advance tax for tax year 2026-27 falls under the 2025 Act, with the instalment dates unchanged: 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March. When paying online, select the correct year and section, as a wrong selection needs correction later.
