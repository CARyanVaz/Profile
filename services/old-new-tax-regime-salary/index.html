Old and new tax regimes, and how salary is taxed

Slabs, rebate and deductions for tax year 2026-27, and the treatment of salary and retirement benefits. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai.

Questions on regimes and salary

What are the tax implications under the old and new tax regimes?

The new regime has lower slab rates and a Rs 60,000 rebate up to Rs 12 lakh of income, but allows few deductions. The old regime has higher rates, with deductions such as those formerly under sections 80C and 80D, HRA and home loan interest. The new regime applies unless you opt out. Special-rate income, such as capital gains on shares, is taxed the same way in both.

When should an individual opt for the old tax regime instead of the new regime?

When your deductions and exemptions are large enough to bring the old regime's tax below the new regime's. This usually needs a combination such as HRA, section 80C investments, health insurance and home loan interest. Work out both every year with your actual figures. You can try the regime calculator.

What deductions are available under the new tax regime?

The main ones are the Rs 75,000 standard deduction for salary and pension, the employer's NPS contribution up to 14% of basic and DA, a family pension deduction up to Rs 25,000, and the Agniveer Corpus Fund. Retirement exemptions, such as gratuity, also stay. Interest on a let-out property reduces its rent, but a loss cannot be set against other income.

How is the tax rebate calculated for an individual taxpayer?

A resident individual gets a rebate up to Rs 60,000 under the new regime if total income does not exceed Rs 12 lakh, and up to Rs 12,500 under the old regime if it does not exceed Rs 5 lakh. It reduces tax before cess (section 156, formerly section 87A). It does not apply against tax on income at special rates, such as gains on listed shares. Non-residents do not get it.

How is marginal relief calculated?

Under the new regime, if income is slightly above Rs 12 lakh, tax is limited to the amount by which income exceeds Rs 12 lakh. On Rs 12.10 lakh, slab tax is Rs 61,500, but you pay only Rs 10,000 plus cess. Marginal relief also applies at the surcharge levels of Rs 50 lakh and Rs 1 crore and above, so extra tax does not exceed the extra income.

What is the tax treatment of salary, bonus, incentives and perquisites?

Salary is taxed when due or when paid, whichever is earlier. Bonus and incentives are taxed in the year of receipt or when they become due. Perquisites are valued under the rules: the Income-tax Rules, 2026 revised some values from 1 April 2026, including tax-free meals up to Rs 200. Check the current company car and other values with payroll, and the Form 130 issued (formerly Form 16).

What is the tax treatment of employer contributions to PF, NPS and superannuation?

Employer PF up to 12% of salary is not taxed. Employer NPS is deductible up to 14% of basic and DA under the new regime, and 10% for private employees under the old. Employer contributions to PF, NPS and superannuation above Rs 7.5 lakh a year together are taxable, with the income on that excess. Interest on employee PF contributions above Rs 2.5 lakh a year is taxable too.

How are leave encashment and gratuity taxed?

Leave encashed during service is taxable. At retirement or resignation, government employees are fully exempt. Others are exempt up to the lowest of the actual amount, Rs 25 lakh over a lifetime, 10 months' average salary, and unused leave value. Government gratuity is fully exempt. Others are exempt up to Rs 20 lakh, subject to a formula based on salary and years of service.

What are the tax implications of voluntary retirement compensation?

Up to Rs 5 lakh is exempt, once in a lifetime, if the scheme meets the conditions in the rules, which include 10 years of service or age 40. The excess is taxed as salary. If you take this exemption, you cannot also claim arrears relief for the same amount. The exemption stays available under the new regime.

Can an individual change the tax regime after filing the original return?

A person without business income chooses in the return, and can generally change it in a revised return only up to the original due date. After that date, including in a belated return, the new regime applies. A person with business income needs Form 10-IEA or its current equivalent by the due date, and can return to the old regime only once. Check the position for your year before relying on this.
