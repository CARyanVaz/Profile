Services / Financial modelling

Financial modelling and projections

Structured spreadsheet models that turn business assumptions into projected profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow and valuation. A model is useful only if others can follow it and test it.

Scope

What the work covers

Banks, investors and boards all ask the same questions in different forms: what will the business earn, how much cash will it need, and can it repay what it borrows? A well-built model answers these questions and shows how the answers change when assumptions change.

Models are built with inputs, calculations and outputs kept separate, clear labelling, and checks that confirm the balance sheet balances and cash reconciles. That makes them easy to review and update.

  • Integrated three-statement models (profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow)
  • Project finance models with debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) and loan repayment schedules
  • Projections and CMA data for bank term loans and working capital limits
  • Business plans and financial projections for investor discussions
  • Budgets, scenarios and sensitivity analysis
  • Valuation support using discounted cash flow
  • Review of existing models for errors, broken links and logic

Approach

How the work is done

Agree the purpose

Who will use the model and for what decision decides its structure, level of detail and time period.

Gather assumptions

Volumes, prices, costs, capital expenditure and funding are agreed with management and recorded with their source.

Build and check

The model is built with integrity checks throughout, then tested with alternative scenarios.

Explain

A short note explains the key assumptions and results, so the model can be presented to a bank or investor with confidence.

Getting started

Records to keep ready

  • Historical financial statements for two to three years, where available
  • Project cost estimates and quotations, for new projects
  • Any lender or investor requirements on format
  • Management's view of volumes, prices and costs

Questions about financial modelling

What is a three-statement financial model?

It is a model in which the projected profit and loss account, balance sheet and cash flow statement are linked, so that a change in one assumption flows through all three. It is the standard format for bank and investor projections.

What is DSCR and why do banks ask for it?

The debt service coverage ratio compares cash available for debt service with the principal and interest due in a period. Banks use it to judge whether a project or business can repay a loan, and usually require a minimum average ratio over the loan term.

Can you review a model someone else built?

Yes. A model review checks formulas, links, assumptions and integrity, and reports errors and risks before the model is relied on for a decision or submitted to a lender.

Which software is used for financial models?

Most models are built in Microsoft Excel because lenders and investors expect it. Google Sheets can be used where collaboration matters more, and Python is used for heavier scenario analysis.
