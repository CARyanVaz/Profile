Foreign companies and businesses in India

Chartered accountancy support for foreign companies and overseas founders entering India or already operating here: choice of entity, foreign exchange reporting, income tax, transfer pricing, GST, accounts and annual compliance. The work is led by Ryan Vaz, FCA, who has held CFO and finance controller roles in India, Uganda and Tanzania.

WhatsAppEmail ryan@vazassociates.in

Scope

What the work covers

From the first decision on structure to the yearly filings. Each engagement is scoped in writing.

Choosing the entity and setting it up

Comparing the options, then completing the registrations for the one chosen.

  • Comparison of subsidiary, LLP, branch, liaison and project office
  • Company or LLP incorporation, with a company secretary where needed
  • Branch, liaison or project office applications through the authorised dealer bank
  • Form FC-1 registration of a foreign company's place of business
  • PAN, TAN, bank account and other registrations

FEMA and RBI reporting

Reporting foreign investment and keeping the records banks and the RBI ask for.

  • Foreign investment rules for your sector
  • Share valuation certificates for issue or transfer of shares
  • Form FC-GPR for shares issued to foreign investors
  • Form FC-TRS for transfers between residents and non-residents
  • Annual FLA return and annual activity certificates

Income tax

Corporate tax for the Indian entity, and Indian tax on cross-border payments.

  • Corporate tax returns, advance tax and tax audit
  • TDS on salaries, vendors and payments to non-residents
  • Treaty relief with Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41 (formerly 10F)
  • Remittance Forms 145 and 146 (formerly 15CA and 15CB)
  • Lower deduction certificates, Form 128 (formerly 13)
  • Replies to notices and permanent establishment review

Transfer pricing

Pricing and records for dealings between the Indian entity and its group.

  • Review of intercompany service, royalty, loan and goods transactions
  • Benchmarking and transfer pricing documentation
  • Accountant's report in Form 48 (formerly 3CEB)
  • Intercompany agreements reviewed with your lawyers

GST

Registration and returns for the Indian entity, kept separate from income tax.

  • GST registration and returns
  • Export of services and LUT
  • Reverse charge on imported services
  • Input tax credit reconciliation and refunds

Accounts, payroll and reporting

Monthly finance work in India, reported in the form your head office needs.

  • Bookkeeping and month-end close
  • Payroll, provident fund, ESIC and professional tax
  • Annual financial statements under Indian standards
  • Group reporting packs and reconciliation to parent's policies
  • Statutory audit is carried out by an independent auditor

Approach

How the work is done

Understand the plan in India

A call covers what the business will sell or do in India, who will own it, who will work here, and how money will move between India and the parent.

Compare the options

The routes are compared on sector rules, tax, repatriation and exit, and the steps and documents for the chosen route are set out in writing.

Complete the registrations

Filings are prepared and tracked, with a company secretary and lawyers involved where the work needs them.

Keep the yearly cycle running

Monthly accounts, tax, GST, payroll and foreign exchange filings follow a calendar, with a report to head office in your format and time zone.

What stays with other professionals

Legal advice and sector licences stay with your lawyers. The statutory audit is carried out by an independent auditor. Filings in your home country stay with your local advisers, and the work here supports them with reconciled numbers.

Questions about foreign companies in India

Which structure should a foreign company use in India?

It depends on what the business will do in India. A private limited subsidiary suits most operating businesses because it can carry on the full range of permitted activities and is taxed as an Indian company. A liaison office only represents the parent and cannot earn income. A branch office can carry on specified activities and is taxed as part of the foreign company. A project office is tied to a particular contract in India. The options are compared on sector rules, tax, repatriation and exit before a decision is taken.

Can a foreign company own 100% of an Indian company?

In many sectors, yes, under the automatic route of the foreign direct investment rules. Some sectors have caps or conditions, some are prohibited, and some need government approval. Investors from countries sharing a land border with India have separate approval requirements. A private limited company needs at least two shareholders, so a nominee commonly holds one share for the parent.

Does an Indian subsidiary need a director living in India?

Yes. A private limited company needs at least two directors, and at least one director must have stayed in India for 182 days or more during the financial year, under section 149(3) of the Companies Act, 2013.

What has to be reported after foreign money comes into the Indian company?

The money must come through banking channels, and shares must be issued within the time allowed. The allotment is reported in Form FC-GPR through the authorised dealer bank within 30 days of allotment. A Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) return is then filed with the RBI by 15 July each year.

How is tax handled on payments to the foreign parent?

The Indian payer generally deducts tax under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 195), at the rate in the Act or the tax treaty, where the treaty is available and beneficial. A treaty claim needs a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F). Remittances are supported by Form 145 and, where required, the accountant's certificate in Form 146 (formerly Forms 15CA and 15CB). Payments between related parties also have to meet transfer pricing rules.

Can this be handled without being in India?

Largely, yes. Calls, document review, filings and accounting are handled remotely. Founders' identity and company documents usually have to be notarised or apostilled in the home country and sent to India, and the required signatures are obtained digitally where the law allows.
