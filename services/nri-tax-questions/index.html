NRI tax questions: status, returns, property, banking and DTAA

Answers to the questions NRIs most often ask about Indian income tax. Written by Ryan Vaz, FCA, a Fellow Chartered Accountant in Borivali West, Mumbai, under the Income-tax Act, 2025. For work on a matter in Borivali, Mumbai or from abroad, see NRI taxation.

Residential status

NRI residential status and income tax

Full guide: residential status, the 120-day rule and RNOR, with a worked example →

Who qualifies as an NRI under Indian Income Tax rules?

Tax law uses the term non-resident. Under section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, an individual is non-resident for a tax year if none of the residence tests is met. The basic tests are 182 days or more in India in the year, or 60 days in the year plus 365 days over the preceding four years. Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin have relaxed tests. FEMA has its own separate test, which governs bank accounts.

How many days can an NRI stay in India without becoming a resident?

For an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin on a visit, the usual limit is under 182 days in the tax year. If Indian income, excluding foreign sources, exceeds Rs 15 lakh, you become resident at 120 days if you also spent 365 days in India over the preceding four years. An Indian citizen with Indian income above Rs 15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any other country can be deemed resident regardless of days.

What happens if an NRI stays in India for more than 182 days?

At 182 days or more in a tax year, you are resident for that year. You may still be Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR), in which case most foreign income stays outside Indian tax. Once you are resident and ordinarily resident, your worldwide income is taxable and foreign assets must be reported in the return. Your bank account status follows FEMA, not this test.

How is NRI residential status calculated?

Count the days of physical presence in India in the tax year, 1 April to 31 March, and the days in the preceding four years, using passport stamps. Apply the 182-day and 60-day tests with the relaxations for citizens and persons of Indian origin. If you are resident, then check RNOR: non-resident in 9 of the preceding 10 years, or 729 days or less in India in the preceding 7 years. Status is decided afresh each year.

What is the difference between NRI, RNOR and Resident?

A non-resident is taxed in India only on income earned, received or arising in India. An RNOR is also generally taxed on Indian income only, plus foreign income from a business controlled or profession set up in India. A resident and ordinarily resident is taxed on worldwide income, can claim foreign tax credit, and must report foreign assets in Schedule FA.

Does an NRI have to pay income tax in India?

Yes, on income earned or received in India, such as rent, capital gains on Indian assets, NRO interest, dividends and Indian salary. NRE interest is exempt while you are a person resident outside India under FEMA. The rebate that makes income up to a set level tax-free for residents is not available to non-residents, so tax can arise on smaller amounts.

Is foreign salary taxable in India for an NRI?

Salary for work done outside India and received outside India is not taxable in India for a non-resident. Salary for work done in India is taxable, wherever it is paid. Salary first received in India, including into an NRE account directly from an employer, can also become taxable, so have it paid abroad and then remit it.

Is income earned outside India taxable in India?

Not for a non-resident, unless it is received or deemed to be received in India. The same generally holds for an RNOR, except income from a business controlled or a profession set up in India. Money you remit to India from income already earned abroad is not income when it arrives.

What happens to my tax status when I return permanently to India?

Income tax status changes by the day count, and is often RNOR for the first years, so foreign income can stay outside Indian tax for that period. It then becomes resident and ordinarily resident. FEMA status changes earlier, from the day you return for work, business or an uncertain stay, and your NRE and NRO accounts must be redesignated. See returning to India.

How does the new Income Tax Act, 2025 affect NRIs?

The Act applies from 1 April 2026 and uses tax year in place of previous year and assessment year. The residence tests in section 6 are carried over. Many section and form numbers change: for example, TDS on non-residents is section 393(2) (formerly section 195), treaty relief is section 159 (formerly section 90), and Form 10F is now Form 41. Income up to 31 March 2026 stays under the old Act.

Filing returns

NRI income tax return filing

Is it mandatory for an NRI to file an income tax return in India?

It is required if total Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit, which for FY 2025-26 is Rs 4 lakh under the new regime. You must also file to claim a TDS refund, and usually after selling property. Where the only income is investment income or specified long-term gains with full tax deducted, filing may not be required (section 216, formerly section 115G).

How can an NRI file an income tax return from abroad?

Register on the income tax e-filing portal with your PAN, overseas mobile and email. Download AIS and Form 26AS, work out Indian income and TDS, select non-resident status and give the days spent in India. File online and verify the return. A pre-validated Indian bank account is needed for a refund.

Which ITR form should an NRI use?

Most NRIs use ITR-2, which covers salary, house property, capital gains and other sources. ITR-3 applies if there is business or professional income in India. ITR-1 and ITR-4 are for residents only. Filing the wrong form can lead to a defective return notice and delay any refund.

Can an NRI file an income tax return without visiting India?

Yes. Filing and verification are done online, and most notices are answered online too. You do not need to travel or give a power of attorney to file a return, though your Indian mobile or bank details make verification easier.

Can a Chartered Accountant file my Indian tax return while I am overseas?

Yes. A chartered accountant can prepare the computation from your documents and file it through the portal, with your authorisation. You still verify the return yourself, and you remain responsible for its contents.

What documents are required for NRI income tax return filing?

PAN and portal login, passport pages showing travel dates, AIS and Form 26AS, and TDS certificates. Add NRO and NRE statements with interest certificates, rent agreements, capital gains statements, purchase and sale deeds for property, and a Tax Residency Certificate with Form 41 if claiming treaty relief. Also keep details of an Indian bank account for the refund.

What is the due date for NRI income tax return filing?

For FY 2025-26, the due date was 31 July 2026 for most individuals without business income and 31 August 2026 for business cases not subject to audit. Audit cases have until 21 November 2026. A belated return can be filed up to 31 December 2026 with a late fee. See the compliance calendar for current dates.

Can an NRI claim a refund of excess TDS?

Yes, by filing the return for that year and showing the actual tax on your income. This is common on property sales, NRO interest and mutual fund gains, because TDS is often higher than the final tax. The refund goes to a pre-validated bank account in your name, so validate the account before filing.

How can an NRI verify an income tax return from abroad?

Use an electronic verification code through a pre-validated Indian bank or demat account, net banking, or Aadhaar OTP if your Aadhaar is linked to a working mobile. Otherwise, sign the ITR-V and send it by post to CPC, Bengaluru, to reach within 30 days of filing. A return that is not verified is treated as not filed.

What happens if an NRI does not file an income tax return?

You lose any refund of excess TDS, and some losses cannot be carried forward. If tax is due, interest and a late fee of up to Rs 5,000 apply, and the department may issue a notice. For a missed year, an updated return can be filed within the time allowed, with additional tax. See late returns.

Property

NRI property transactions in India

Full guide: TDS when an NRI sells property, with a worked example →

What is the tax on the sale of property by an NRI in India?

Property held for more than 24 months gives a long-term gain, taxed at 12.5% without indexation, plus surcharge and cess. Property held for 24 months or less gives a short-term gain, taxed at slab rates. The option of the older indexed method for property bought before 23 July 2024 is limited to residents. Exemptions for reinvestment may reduce the tax.

How much TDS should be deducted when buying property from an NRI?

On the full sale price, under section 393(2): 12.5% plus surcharge and cess for a long-term gain, which comes to 13% to 14.95%, and 30% plus surcharge and cess for a short-term gain. The Rs 50 lakh limit for resident sellers does not apply. From 1 October 2026, resident individual and HUF buyers can use PAN-based Form 141 without a TAN. Try the calculator.

Can an NRI avoid excessive TDS on the sale of property?

Yes, by obtaining a lower or nil deduction certificate before the sale, because TDS is otherwise on the price and not on the gain. The buyer then deducts at the rate in the certificate. Without a certificate, the excess can only be recovered through a refund after filing the return.

How can an NRI obtain a lower TDS certificate?

Apply online in Form 128 (formerly Form 13) under section 395 on TRACES, with a capital gains computation, purchase and sale documents, the buyer's details and earlier returns. Your PAN and TRACES registration must be in order. The officer can raise queries, and it can take several weeks, so apply well before the sale date.

How is capital gains tax calculated on the sale of property by an NRI?

Start with the sale price, or the stamp duty value if higher beyond the permitted margin. Deduct transfer costs such as brokerage, then the purchase cost and improvement cost. For property bought before 1 April 2001, fair market value on that date can replace cost. There is no indexation for non-residents on current sales. Then apply 12.5% or slab rates, plus surcharge and cess.

Can an NRI claim exemption under Section 54?

Yes. An NRI can claim the exemption now in section 82 (formerly section 54) on a long-term gain from a residential house by buying a house in India within one year before or two years after the sale, or building one within three years. It is capped at Rs 10 crore. Section 85 (formerly section 54EC) allows up to Rs 50 lakh in specified bonds within six months. Unused amounts go to a capital gains account by the return due date.

What happens if the buyer deducts excess TDS?

The seller claims the excess as a refund by filing the return for that year. Check that the buyer has filed the TDS statement and that the credit appears in Form 26AS. If the buyer has deducted but not reported it correctly, the buyer must correct the statement, because credit generally follows Form 26AS.

How can an NRI get a refund of TDS deducted on property sale?

File an income tax return for the year of sale showing the actual capital gain and any exemption claimed. Keep the purchase and sale deeds and proof of costs. Pre-validate an Indian bank account for the refund and verify the return. The refund comes after processing, and any queries are raised online.

Can an NRI sell inherited property in India?

Yes. For capital gains, the previous owner's cost and holding period are counted, so most inherited property gives a long-term gain. Keep the will or succession document and the original purchase papers. TDS applies in the usual way. Proceeds can be remitted from the NRO account within the USD 1 million yearly limit.

How can an NRI repatriate property sale proceeds abroad?

Credit the proceeds to your NRO account and remit up to USD 1 million per financial year, after paying tax. Your bank will generally need Forms 145 and 146 (formerly 15CA and 15CB). Where the property was bought from NRE or foreign funds, separate repatriation rules apply, limited to the foreign exchange paid and to two residential properties. Check the details with your bank first.

NRE and NRO

NRE, NRO and banking taxation

Is interest earned on an NRE account taxable in India?

No, while you are a person resident outside India under FEMA. The exemption stops once you become a FEMA resident, even if you are still RNOR for income tax. It may be taxable in your country of residence, so check the rules there.

Is interest on an NRO account taxable?

Yes. It is taxable in India, and banks generally deduct TDS at 30% plus surcharge and cess. A lower rate may apply under a tax treaty if you give the bank a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41. You can claim any excess back by filing a return.

What is the difference between NRE and NRO accounts?

An NRE account holds foreign earnings in rupees, is fully repatriable, and its interest is exempt in India. An NRO account holds income earned in India, such as rent and dividends. Its interest is taxable, and repatriation is limited to USD 1 million a financial year. NRE accounts can be joint only with another NRI, or with a resident on a former-or-survivor basis.

Can an NRI claim a refund of TDS deducted on NRO interest?

Yes, if your actual tax is lower than the TDS, by filing a return for the year. This often helps where total Indian income is small or a treaty rate applies. Check that the bank's TDS appears in Form 26AS before filing.

Can an NRI transfer money from an NRO account to an overseas account?

Yes, up to USD 1 million per financial year, for current income and sale proceeds, once tax has been paid. Your bank will generally ask for Forms 145 and 146 (formerly 15CA and 15CB). Rent, dividends and similar current income can usually be remitted within this process.

What are the tax implications of converting an NRO account into an NRE account?

An NRO account is not converted into an NRE account as such. You can transfer funds from NRO to NRE only within the USD 1 million yearly limit, after tax is paid, with the same forms as a remittance. The transfer itself is not income. Interest on that money becomes exempt once it is in the NRE account.

Is FCNR deposit interest taxable in India?

No, while you are a non-resident or RNOR for income tax. An FCNR(B) deposit is held in foreign currency for one to five years, and principal and interest are repatriable. The exemption ends once you become resident and ordinarily resident. Check whether your country of residence taxes it.

Can an NRI maintain a savings account in India after moving abroad?

Not as a resident account. Once you are a person resident outside India under FEMA, an existing resident savings account should be redesignated as NRO. Tell your bank when you leave. You can then keep NRO for Indian income and NRE for foreign earnings.

DTAA

DTAA: double taxation relief

Can an NRI be taxed in two countries on the same income?

Yes. India may tax income arising in India, and your country of residence may tax your worldwide income. A tax treaty decides which country can tax and how much, and the country of residence usually gives credit for tax paid in India.

How can an NRI avoid double taxation?

Pay the lower treaty rate in India where one applies, using a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41. Then claim credit for the Indian tax in your country of residence, under its rules. Keep Form 26AS and TDS certificates as proof, as that country will usually ask for them.

What is a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement?

It is a treaty between India and another country that allocates taxing rights on income such as interest, dividends, royalties, salary and capital gains. Under section 159 (formerly section 90), you can use the treaty or Indian law, whichever is more beneficial. India has treaties with many countries, including the UAE, USA, UK, Singapore and Canada.

How can an NRI claim DTAA benefits in India?

Get a Tax Residency Certificate from your country of residence and e-file Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) where needed. Give these to the bank or payer before TDS is deducted, and claim the relief in your return, which also reports the treaty article relied on. If TDS has already been deducted at the higher rate, recover the excess through the return.

What is a Tax Residency Certificate?

It is a certificate from the tax authority of your country of residence, confirming that you are resident there for a stated period. It is required to claim treaty relief in India. Obtain one for each year in which you claim relief, because Indian payers and the department will ask for it.

Is Form 10F mandatory for NRIs?

Form 10F is now Form 41 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. It is needed with a Tax Residency Certificate when claiming treaty relief, if the certificate does not contain all the required details, and is filed online with your PAN. It is not needed if you are not claiming treaty relief.

Can an NRI claim foreign tax credit in India?

Generally no, because a non-resident is taxed in India only on Indian income. Foreign tax credit is mainly for residents taxed in India on foreign income, who claim it in Form 44 (formerly Form 67). An NRI usually claims credit for Indian tax in the country of residence.

What happens if tax has already been paid in the country of residence?

It does not by itself reduce Indian tax on income arising in India. The treaty decides which country has the right to tax. If India has the right, your country of residence usually gives credit. If only the other country has the right, claim relief in your Indian return with a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41.

Investments

NRI investments and capital gains

How are mutual fund investments taxed for NRIs?

For equity funds, gains held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh a year, and gains held for less at 20%. Debt fund units bought from 1 April 2023 are generally taxed at slab rates. TDS is deducted on redemption, on the whole gain, so the Rs 1.25 lakh relief often has to be claimed through the return. Surcharge and cess apply.

What is the capital gains tax on shares for NRIs?

On listed shares sold on an exchange, gains held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh a year, and gains held for 12 months or less at 20%. Unlisted shares are long-term after 24 months and taxed at 12.5%; if held for less, they are taxed at slab rates. Surcharge and cess apply. A tax treaty may change the result.

Can NRIs invest in Indian mutual funds?

Yes, using an NRE or NRO account, with KYC showing NRI status. Investments from NRE funds are generally repatriable. Some fund houses restrict investors from the USA and Canada because of reporting rules there, so check with the fund house first.

What is the tax on dividends received by NRIs?

Dividends from Indian companies and mutual funds are taxable in India, generally at 20% plus surcharge and cess, and TDS is deducted at that rate. A lower treaty rate may apply if you give a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41 to the company or registrar before payment.

Are NRI investments in Indian shares taxable?

Holding shares is not taxed. Dividends and gains on sale are. Rates depend on the type of share and the holding period, and the route used to buy them matters for repatriation, so keep your broker and bank records.

Can an NRI claim deductions under Section 80C?

Yes, under the old regime only. An NRI can claim life insurance premiums, equity-linked savings schemes, children's tuition fees and home loan principal, formerly under section 80C. An NRI cannot open a new PPF account or buy NSC. Under the new regime, which is the default, these deductions are not available.

What are the tax implications of selling inherited shares?

Receiving shares by inheritance is not taxed. When you sell, the previous owner's cost and holding period count, and the usual capital gains rates apply. Keep the transmission documents and the original purchase records. Without them, the gain is hard to support.

How are investments taxed when an NRI returns to India?

Gains on Indian investments continue to be taxed under the usual rules. Your broker, fund houses and banks must be told of the change of status, and NRE-linked holdings moved to resident status. Foreign investments stay outside Indian tax while you are RNOR, and become taxable and reportable once you are resident and ordinarily resident. See returning to India.

Notices

NRI income tax notices and compliance

I am an NRI and have received an income tax notice in India. What should I do?

Note the section, tax year and reply date, and check that it appears on the e-filing portal under Pending Actions. Update your overseas email and mobile there. Compare the items with your AIS, Form 26AS and records, then reply online by the date. If you need more time, ask for it before the date. Do not ignore it, as an order can be passed without your side.

How can an NRI respond to an income tax notice from abroad?

Reply through the e-Proceedings section of the e-filing portal, uploading your explanation and documents. A personal hearing, where available, can generally be held by video. Keep the acknowledgement. An authorised representative can also reply for you.

Why has the Income Tax Department issued a notice to an NRI?

Common reasons include a property sale with no return filed, high-value transactions in AIS, interest or rent not reported, a wrong residential status, foreign remittances, and a claim for TDS or treaty relief not matching the records. Sometimes the notice arises from a wrong address or PAN entry, which also needs correcting.

How can an NRI correct an income tax return?

A revised return can be filed for FY 2025-26 up to 31 December 2026, or up to 31 March 2027 with a late fee. After that, an updated return is available within the time limit, with additional tax, but not to claim a refund or reduce tax. Mistakes apparent from the record can be corrected by rectification under section 287 (formerly section 154).

What happens if TDS deducted from an NRI's income is not reflected in Form 26AS?

Credit may be denied when the return is processed. Ask the deductor, such as the buyer, bank or tenant, to file or correct the TDS statement with your correct PAN. Keep the TDS certificate as evidence. Claim the credit once it appears, or file a response with the proof if a demand is raised.

Can an NRI authorise a Chartered Accountant to represent them before the Income Tax Department?

Yes. A chartered accountant can act as your authorised representative. You add the authorisation on the e-filing portal and give a written authority, and the representative then replies and attends on your behalf. You stay responsible for the facts given, so check replies before they go in.

How can an NRI resolve an income tax refund issue?

Check the refund status on the portal, and confirm that the bank account is pre-validated and that the return has been verified and processed. Respond to any adjustment notice for old demands. If it remains stuck, raise a grievance through the e-Nivaran facility, or with the Centralised Processing Centre, using the return's acknowledgement number.

Can an NRI respond to income tax proceedings without travelling to India?

Yes, in most cases. Assessments and appeals are largely handled online, with replies uploaded and hearings by video where allowed. Your authorised representative can act for you. Travel may be needed only in unusual matters, such as a summons or court proceedings.
