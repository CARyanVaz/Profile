Knowledge and frequently asked questions

Short, factual answers to questions often asked in Mumbai about the GST changes of 2026-27, NRI tax, AIS and Form 26AS, notices, late returns, capital gains, GST, setting up a business and fractional CFO services.

Reviewed by Ryan Vaz, FCA, Certified Forensic Accountant (ICAI). Status dates are shown with each group of answers.

GST changes 2026-27

Status as on 4 November 2026. Changes marked as recommended or expected apply only once notified; these answers will be updated as notifications are issued. See also GST updates by date, GST notices and GSTR-9. NRIs can see NRI tax questions.

What changes in GST from 1 November 2026?

Following the 57th GST Council meeting in October 2026, CBIC issued Notification 03/2026-Central Tax, bringing sections 153 to 155 of the Finance Act, 2026 into force, and Notification 05/2026-Central Tax, the CGST (Amendment) Rules, 2026, both dated 26 October 2026. They apply from 1 November 2026, with some parts from 1 December 2026, 1 January 2027 and 1 April 2027. They cover quicker registration with fewer queries, faster refunds, refund of input tax credit on input services for businesses with an inverted duty structure, and registration of e-commerce sellers using the operator's warehouse. Draft versions of the redesigned GSTR-1, GSTR-1A and IFF forms were published for public comment until 30 November 2026. Changes that need further amendments to the GST Acts are expected from 1 April 2027; CBIC Instruction 02/2026-GST dated 30 October 2026 applies some of them to central GST officers in the meantime. GST services.

Will GST refunds now be paid faster?

Yes, for most claims. Under rules 90 and 91 as amended by Notification 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026, from 1 November 2026 the department must issue an acknowledgement or a deficiency memo within 10 days instead of 15, failing which the application is treated as acknowledged. For exports and other zero-rated supplies and inverted duty claims, 90% of the amount can be sanctioned provisionally through the portal on a risk-based check, within 3 working days of acknowledgement. The revised Form RFD-01 applies from 1 December 2026. The official FAQs on the Council's recommendations say the changes are being phased in as the system becomes ready. An excess balance in the electronic cash ledger is to be refunded automatically. These apply to new claims; claims already pending follow the existing process. GST services.

Can I now claim a refund of GST paid on input services under an inverted duty structure?

Yes, for input tax credit on input services availed on or after 1 November 2026, under rule 89(5) as substituted by Notification 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026. Until now, inverted duty refunds covered only credit on inputs (goods), so service businesses and manufacturers with large service costs could not recover the accumulated credit. Under the same rule, credit on capital goods availed on or after 1 April 2027 is refundable over 60 months. Credit availed before these dates is not covered. GST services.

Will GSTR-3B be locked to GSTR-1 from April 2027?

That is the direction announced. GSTR-1, GSTR-1A and IFF are being redesigned so that the tax liability in GSTR-3B flows from what is declared in them, with new statements for reverse charge tax and input tax credit, a mechanism to correct liability and credit within the return system, and statutory backing for the Invoice Management System. CBIC has said most GST notices arise from mismatches between returns. Businesses should reconcile GSTR-1 with GSTR-3B every month now, and especially clear differences from earlier months. GST services.

Is the power to arrest under GST being removed, and what are the new penalty limits?

The GST Council recommended removing the power to arrest, raising the threshold for prosecution from Rs 1 crore to Rs 5 crore of tax, reducing the general penalty from Rs 50,000 to Rs 20,000 (CGST and SGST together), and allowing a reduced penalty of 5% in non-fraud cases where tax and interest are paid within 30 days of the order. These need amendments to the GST Acts and are expected from 1 April 2027. In the meantime, CBIC Instruction 02/2026-GST dated 30 October 2026 directs central GST officers to make no further arrests under GST until the amendment takes effect. State GST officers are covered only if the State issues a similar instruction. The prosecution threshold and penalty limits do not change until the amendments take effect. GST services.

Will I get a GST show-cause notice for a small amount?

Not from central GST officers, under CBIC Instruction 02/2026-GST dated 30 October 2026. They may not issue a show-cause notice under section 73, 74 or 74A where the amount involved is below Rs 10,000, counting CGST, SGST or UTGST, IGST and compensation cess together. State GST officers are covered only if the State issues a similar instruction. The instruction is interim and does not deal with notices already issued. The GST Council also recommended an amendment to the law, expected from 1 April 2027, which is to apply to notices and appeals pending when it takes effect. Until then, any notice you receive must be answered in time. GST services.

What is the proposed annual return with quarterly payment (ARQP) scheme?

The GST Council approved in principle an optional scheme for businesses with turnover up to Rs 5 crore in the previous year that supply only to unregistered customers, such as retailers and consumer service businesses. They would pay tax quarterly and file one annual return instead of monthly returns. The scheme has not been notified yet; the conditions, rates of interest and forms will be known only when it is. GST services.

Is the GSTR-3B late fee being waived for small businesses?

The GST Council recommended that a business with turnover up to Rs 5 crore should pay no late fee if a delayed GSTR-3B is filed by the end of the month in which it was due. This is a recommendation and applies only once notified. Interest at 18% a year on tax paid late still applies. Use the late payment interest calculator to work it out. GST services.

Can an e-commerce seller register in another state using the platform's warehouse?

Yes, from 1 November 2026, under new rule 14B of the CGST Rules (Notification 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026). A person registered in a home state who supplies goods through an e-commerce operator can register in a state where they have no physical presence and declare the operator's warehouse there as the principal place of business. Output tax on supplies to registered persons, excluding those on the same PAN, must not exceed Rs 2.5 lakh a month. The operator must consent within 7 working days, Aadhaar authentication is required, and the registration is for supplies through that platform only. The application is made on the GST portal in Part B of Form GST REG-01 (GSTN Advisory No. 682 dated 1 November 2026). GST services.

NRI taxation

Status as on 2 November 2026.

Who is treated as a non-resident (NRI) for Indian income tax?

Under section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, an individual is resident if in India for 182 days or more in the tax year, or for 60 days in the year plus 365 days over the preceding four years. For Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin visiting India, the 60-day test becomes 182 days, or 120 days if Indian income exceeds Rs 15 lakh. An Indian citizen with Indian income above Rs 15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any other country can be deemed resident. Anyone meeting none of these tests is non-resident. NRI taxation.

Is interest on NRE and NRO accounts taxable in India?

Interest on an NRE account is exempt in India while the holder is a person resident outside India under FEMA (formerly section 10(4)(ii)). Interest on an NRO account is taxable, and banks generally deduct tax at 30% plus surcharge and cess under section 393(2) (formerly section 195). A lower treaty rate may apply under section 159 if the depositor provides a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F). What happens to these accounts when you return to India.

What tax is deducted when an NRI sells property in Mumbai?

The buyer must deduct tax on the full sale price, not only on the capital gain, under section 393(2) (formerly section 195), and the Rs 50 lakh threshold for resident sellers does not apply. For a long-term gain the rate is 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, which is 13% to 14.95% of the price; for a short-term gain it is 30% plus surcharge and cess. On a sale price of Rs 1.2 crore with a long-term gain, TDS at 14.95% is Rs 17,94,000. From 1 October 2026, a resident individual or HUF buyer can use the PAN-based Form 141 without a TAN (Notification 121/2026); other buyers still need a TAN. The seller can apply in Form 128 (formerly Form 13) for a lower deduction certificate if the actual tax on the gain is lower. Use the TDS on NRI property calculator.

Does an NRI need to file an income tax return in India?

An NRI must file a return under section 263 if total Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit, and must also file to claim a refund of excess tax deducted. Where the only income is investment income or specified long-term capital gains on which full tax was deducted, filing may not be required (section 216, formerly section 115G). Property sales usually need a return to settle the actual tax. NRI taxation.

AIS, Form 26AS and income tax

Status as on 2 November 2026.

What is the difference between AIS and Form 26AS?

Form 26AS mainly shows tax deducted or collected at source, advance tax and self-assessment tax against your PAN. The Annual Information Statement (section 510) is broader and also reports interest, dividends, securities and mutual fund transactions, rent and foreign remittances. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, Form 26AS is renumbered Form 168 from tax year 2026-27. AIS and Form 26AS reconciliation.

Why does my AIS not match Form 26AS or my own records?

Common reasons include AIS showing the gross sale value of shares rather than the gain, interest reported on accrual while your passbook shows credits, duplicate entries from different reporting entities, and transactions reported under the wrong PAN. You can submit feedback on each AIS entry on the income tax portal, and the reporting entity may then correct it. AIS and Form 26AS reconciliation.

What happens if income shown in AIS is not reflected in my return?

The department compares returns with AIS data. Unexplained differences can lead to an intimation adjusting your income, a notice, or an e-campaign communication asking for a response. Reconciling AIS, Form 26AS and your books before filing, and recording feedback on entries that are incorrect, reduces the risk of such mismatches. AIS and Form 26AS reconciliation.

GST reconciliation

Status as on 2 November 2026.

What is GSTR-2B reconciliation and why does it matter now?

GSTR-2B is the monthly auto-generated statement of input tax credit available from suppliers' filings and the Invoice Management System. Reconciling it with your purchase register identifies missing, mismatched or unfiled supplier invoices. Credit is available only where the supplier has furnished the invoice and it is communicated to you (section 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act), and the credit table of GSTR-3B is auto-populated from GSTR-2B, so differences should be resolved before filing, not after. GST reconciliation and notices.

How should differences between GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and the books be handled?

Compare outward supplies in the books with GSTR-1, and tax liability with GSTR-3B, every month. Since the July 2025 tax period, the liability auto-populated in GSTR-3B from GSTR-1, GSTR-1A and IFF cannot be edited in GSTR-3B (GSTN advisory of 7 June 2025), so errors should be corrected through GSTR-1A before GSTR-3B is filed, or in later returns. Short-paid tax should be paid with interest under section 50 of the CGST Act. Differences left unresolved commonly surface in the annual return, GSTR-9 and 9C, or in departmental scrutiny. Use the late payment interest calculator. GST reconciliation and notices.

Fractional CFO services

Status as on 2 November 2026.

What is a fractional CFO?

A fractional CFO is an experienced finance professional who works with a business for a defined number of days or hours rather than full time. The role usually covers cash flow planning, budgeting, management reporting, lender and investor information, internal controls and finance systems, for businesses needing senior finance input without a full-time appointment. Income tax: notices, late filing and capital gains

Status as on 2 November 2026.

I received an intimation after my return was processed, and the demand is wrong. What should I do?

Compare the intimation (formerly under section 143(1)) line by line with your return; most wrong demands come from TDS not matched, a deduction or relief disallowed by the system, or a mismatch with AIS. On the income tax portal, submit a response to the outstanding demand stating that you disagree and why, and file an online rectification request with the supporting details. If rectification does not resolve it, an appeal can be filed within the time stated in the order. Do not ignore it: an unpaid demand can be adjusted against your future refunds. Income tax services.

My return was marked defective. How do I respond?

A defective return notice (formerly section 139(9)) points out an error such as a missing schedule, tax payable but not paid, or income from business shown without the required accounts. The defect must be removed by filing a corrected return in response to the notice, usually within 15 days, and an extension can be requested on the portal. If it is not corrected in time, the return is treated as if it was never filed, with the consequences of a missed return. Income tax services.

Can I still file my return after the due date, and what does it cost?

A belated return can be filed up to nine months from the end of the tax year, which for FY 2025-26 is 31 December 2026. The late fee is Rs 5,000, or Rs 1,000 where total income does not exceed Rs 5 lakh, plus interest on any tax unpaid. After that, an updated return can be filed up to 48 months from the end of the following year, with additional tax of 25%, 50%, 60% or 70% of the tax and interest depending on how late it is filed. Some losses cannot be carried forward from a late return. See the compliance calendar.

How is tax worked out when I sell a house, and how can I save it?

A house held for more than 24 months gives a long-term gain, taxed at 12.5% without indexation; a resident individual or HUF who bought before 23 July 2024 can instead choose 20% with indexation, whichever is lower. Held for 24 months or less, the gain is added to income at slab rates. Long-term gain can be saved by buying or building another residential house in India within the time limits (exemption capped at Rs 10 crore), or by investing up to Rs 50 lakh in specified capital gains bonds within six months, locked in for five years. Amounts not reinvested before the return due date can be parked in the Capital Gains Account Scheme. For sellers who are NRIs, see NRI taxation.

How are gains on shares and mutual funds taxed?

For listed shares and equity mutual funds on which STT is paid, gains on units held up to 12 months are taxed at 20%, and longer-held gains at 12.5% on the amount above Rs 1.25 lakh a year. Debt mutual funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rates however long they are held. Unlisted shares held for more than 24 months are taxed at 12.5%. The gains are reported in the capital gains schedule of ITR-2 or ITR-3, and AIS shows the sale value, which must be matched with your own cost records. Income tax services.

Is a gift from a relative or friend taxable?

Gifts of money or property from relatives, as defined in the income tax law (spouse, parents, children, siblings and their spouses, and certain other close relatives), are not taxed in the receiver's hands, wherever the relative lives. Gifts received on marriage, by will or inheritance are also exempt. Gifts from anyone else are fully taxable as income if their total value in the year exceeds Rs 50,000. Keep a gift deed or letter and bank records, especially for gifts from NRI relatives. NRI taxation.

Starting a business and GST

Status as on 2 November 2026.

Should I choose a proprietorship, an LLP or a private limited company?

A proprietorship is the simplest to start and is taxed at the owner's slab rates, but the owner is personally liable. An LLP limits liability, is taxed at 30% plus surcharge and cess, and needs an audit only above set turnover or contribution limits. A private limited company limits liability, can raise equity and issue shares to employees, can opt for a lower tax rate of 22% plus surcharge and cess, but must have its accounts audited every year and file more returns with the ROC. The right choice depends on liability, funding plans, the number of owners and the cost of compliance. Accounts and bookkeeping.

What is the GST composition scheme, and who can opt for it?

The composition scheme lets small businesses pay GST at a fixed low rate on turnover and file simpler returns: 1% for traders and manufacturers, 5% for restaurants, and 6% for eligible service providers. It is available to businesses with turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore in the previous year (Rs 50 lakh for service providers). A composition dealer cannot charge GST on invoices, cannot claim input tax credit, and cannot make inter-state supplies of goods. It suits businesses selling mainly to consumers. GST services.

Is GST payable on rent?

Renting a residential unit for use as a residence is exempt, except where it is let to a GST-registered person, who then pays 18% GST under reverse charge. Renting commercial property is taxable at 18% if the landlord is registered; where the landlord is unregistered and the tenant is registered, the tenant pays the tax under reverse charge. A landlord whose rent and other taxable supplies exceed Rs 20 lakh a year must register. For housing society maintenance charges, see housing society services.

Who has to issue e-invoices under GST?

E-invoicing applies to a business whose aggregate turnover has exceeded Rs 5 crore in any financial year since 2017-18, for invoices and credit and debit notes issued to registered buyers and for exports. Once applicable, it continues even if turnover later falls. Businesses with turnover of Rs 10 crore or more must report invoices on the invoice registration portal within 30 days of the invoice date. A buyer cannot claim input tax credit on an invoice that should have been an e-invoice but was not. GST services.

What is Udyam registration, and what is the 45-day payment rule?

Udyam registration is the free, online MSME registration on the government portal, using Aadhaar and PAN. Since April 2025, a micro enterprise has investment up to Rs 2.5 crore and turnover up to Rs 10 crore, and a small enterprise up to Rs 25 crore and Rs 100 crore. Buyers must pay registered micro and small suppliers within the agreed credit period, not exceeding 45 days, or 15 days where none is agreed; otherwise the buyer cannot deduct the expense for tax until the year it is paid (formerly section 43B(h)), and owes interest to the supplier.
